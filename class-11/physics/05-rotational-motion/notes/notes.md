## 🎯 Quick summary (the whole topic in a nutshell)

Rotational motion is just ordinary (linear) mechanics with the names swapped. Once you
know the "dictionary", most of the chapter solves itself:

| Linear idea | Becomes (rotational) |
|---|---|
| mass $m$ | moment of inertia $I$ |
| force $F$ | torque $\tau$ |
| $F = ma$ | $\tau = I\alpha$ |
| momentum $p = mv$ | angular momentum $L = I\omega$ |
| KE $\tfrac12 mv^2$ | $\tfrac12 I\omega^2$ |

**The five things to really understand:**

1. **Moment of inertia ($I$)** is "rotational mass" — how hard it is to spin something.
   It depends on *where* the mass is: mass far from the axis counts much more (it uses
   $r^2$). Learn the standard shapes (ring, disc, rod, sphere) and the two axis theorems.
2. **Torque ($\tau$)** is the "turning force" — force × perpendicular distance from the
   axis. A force pointing straight at the axis does nothing (that's why you push a door
   at its edge, not its hinge).
3. **Angular momentum is conserved when there's no outside turning force.** This is the
   skater-spin idea: pull your arms in ($I$ drops), and you spin faster ($\omega$ rises)
   so that $I\omega$ stays the same. It runs everything from divers to pulsars.
4. **Rolling = moving + spinning at once.** The key link is $v = R\omega$. A rolling
   body carries extra (rotational) energy, so on an incline a solid sphere beats a disc
   beats a ring — and the result doesn't depend on mass or radius, only on the shape.
5. **Pick the right point to take torque about.** Choosing the point where an unknown
   force acts makes that force disappear from the maths. This one habit cracks most of
   the "hard" problems.
