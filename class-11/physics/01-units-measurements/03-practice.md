> (open after a real attempt) and a **collapsible full solution**. Topics: unit conversion, dimensional formulae,
> homogeneity, significant figures, errors & propagation, the dimensional method.

### P12

<details><summary>🧭 Approach</summary>

## 🔴 Advanced (P26–P35)

### P26
> Derive how the orbital period $T$ of a planet depends on orbit radius $r$, mass of sun $M$ and $G$ (dimensional method).

<details><summary>🧭 Approach</summary>

$T = k\,r^a M^b G^c$; $[G] = [\text{M}^{-1}\text{L}^3\text{T}^{-2}]$.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Solution</summary>

$[\text{T}] = [\text{L}]^a[\text{M}]^b[\text{M}^{-1}\text{L}^3\text{T}^{-2}]^c$. T: $1 = -2c \Rightarrow c = -\tfrac12$; M: $0 = b - c \Rightarrow b = -\tfrac12$; L: $0 = a + 3c \Rightarrow a = \tfrac32$. So $T \propto \sqrt{\dfrac{r^3}{GM}}$ (Kepler's third law form).
</details>

---

### P27
> The escape velocity $v$ depends on $g$ (surface gravity) and radius $R$. Find the relation.

<details><summary>🧭 Approach</summary>

$v = k\,g^a R^b$.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Solution</summary>

$[\text{LT}^{-1}] = [\text{LT}^{-2}]^a[\text{L}]^b$. T: $-1 = -2a \Rightarrow a = \tfrac12$; L: $1 = a + b \Rightarrow b = \tfrac12$. So $v \propto \sqrt{gR}$.
</details>

---

### P28
> In $Y = \dfrac{FL}{A\,\Delta L}$ (Young's modulus), $F$, $L$, $A$, $\Delta L$ have errors 2%, 1%, 3% (area), 4%. Find max % error in $Y$.

<details><summary>🧭 Approach</summary>

Sum relative errors (all first power here).
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Solution</summary>

$2 + 1 + 3 + 4 = 10\%$.
</details>

---

### P29
> Find the dimensional formula of the universal gas constant $R$ (from $PV = nRT$).

<details><summary>🧭 Approach</summary>

$R = \frac{PV}{nT}$; $n$ in mol → $[\text{N}]$, $T$ → $[\text{K}]$.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Solution</summary>

$[R] = \dfrac{[\text{ML}^{-1}\text{T}^{-2}][\text{L}^3]}{[\text{N}][\text{K}]} = [\text{ML}^2\text{T}^{-2}\text{K}^{-1}\text{N}^{-1}]$.
</details>

---

### P30
> Why is $F = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$ dimensionally consistent but $F = \dfrac{mv}{r}$ not (for centripetal force)?

<details><summary>🧭 Approach</summary>

Check dimensions of each.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Solution</summary>

$\left[\frac{mv^2}{r}\right] = \frac{[\text{M}][\text{L}^2\text{T}^{-2}]}{[\text{L}]} = [\text{MLT}^{-2}] = [F]$ ✓. $\left[\frac{mv}{r}\right] = \frac{[\text{M}][\text{LT}^{-1}]}{[\text{L}]} = [\text{MT}^{-1}] \ne [F]$. So only the $v^2$ form is valid.
</details>

---

### P31
> A cylinder's volume uses radius $(1.00 \pm 0.01)$ cm and height $(5.0 \pm 0.1)$ cm. Find the % error in $V = \pi r^2 h$.

<details><summary>🧭 Approach</summary>

$\frac{\Delta V}{V} = 2\frac{\Delta r}{r} + \frac{\Delta h}{h}$.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Solution</summary>

$2(1\%) + 2\% = 4\%$.
</details>

---

### P32
> Show that $\dfrac12 mv^2$ and $mgh$ have the same dimensions (energy).

<details><summary>🧭 Approach</summary>

Reduce both.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Solution</summary>

$[mv^2] = [\text{M}][\text{L}^2\text{T}^{-2}] = [\text{ML}^2\text{T}^{-2}]$; $[mgh] = [\text{M}][\text{LT}^{-2}][\text{L}] = [\text{ML}^2\text{T}^{-2}]$. Same. ∎
</details>

---

### P33
> The dimensional formula $[\text{ML}^2\text{T}^{-3}\text{A}^{-1}]$ belongs to which quantity?

<details><summary>🧭 Approach</summary>

Compare with known formulae (power / current = ?).
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Solution</summary>

$\frac{[\text{power}]}{[\text{current}]} = \frac{[\text{ML}^2\text{T}^{-3}]}{[\text{A}]}$ = **electric potential (voltage)**.
</details>

---

### P34
> A student measures $g$ with $\frac{\Delta \ell}{\ell} = 0.5\%$ and $\frac{\Delta T}{T} = 1\%$ using $g = \frac{4\pi^2\ell}{T^2}$. Which measurement should be improved first, and what is the total error?

<details><summary>🧭 Approach</summary>

$\frac{\Delta g}{g} = \frac{\Delta\ell}{\ell} + 2\frac{\Delta T}{T}$; the period enters squared.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Solution</summary>

$0.5\% + 2(1\%) = 2.5\%$. The **period** $T$ dominates (weighted by 2), so improving the timing matters most.
</details>
