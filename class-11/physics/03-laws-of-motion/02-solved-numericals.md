## 🟢 Basic (B1–B10)

<a id="b1"></a>
### B1

> ❓ **Question.** A force of $20\ \mathrm{N}$ acts on a $4\ \mathrm{kg}$ body. Find its acceleration.

**✅ Answer.** $a = F/m = 20/4 = \boxed{5\ \mathrm{m/s^2}}$.

---

<a id="b2"></a>
### B2

> ❓ **Question.** A $2\ \mathrm{kg}$ body moving at $3\ \mathrm{m/s}$ is brought to rest. Find the change in its momentum.

**✅ Answer.** $\Delta p = m(v-u) = 2(0-3) = \boxed{-6\ \mathrm{kg\,m/s}}$ (magnitude $6$, opposite to motion).

---

<a id="b3"></a>
### B3

> ❓ **Question.** A ball of mass $0.1\ \mathrm{kg}$ hits a wall at $10\ \mathrm{m/s}$ and rebounds at $10\ \mathrm{m/s}$. Find the impulse on the ball.

**✅ Answer.** $J = \Delta p = m(v - u) = 0.1(-10 - 10) = \boxed{-2\ \mathrm{N\,s}}$ (magnitude $2\ \mathrm{N\,s}$).

---

<a id="b4"></a>
### B4

> ❓ **Question.** Find the weight of a $10\ \mathrm{kg}$ object and the normal force on it resting on a horizontal floor.

**✅ Answer.** $W = mg = 100\ \mathrm{N}$; on a level floor with no vertical acceleration, $N = W = \boxed{100\ \mathrm{N}}$.

---

<a id="b5"></a>
### B5

> ❓ **Question.** A $5\ \mathrm{kg}$ block on a rough floor ($\mu_k = 0.2$) is pushed horizontally. Find the kinetic friction force.

**✅ Answer.** $f_k = \mu_k N = \mu_k mg = 0.2(5)(10) = \boxed{10\ \mathrm{N}}$.

---

<a id="b6"></a>
### B6

> ❓ **Question.** A $2\ \mathrm{kg}$ block rests on a floor with $\mu_s = 0.4$. What is the maximum horizontal force before it moves?

**✅ Answer.** $f_{s,\max} = \mu_s mg = 0.4(2)(10) = \boxed{8\ \mathrm{N}}$.

---

<a id="b7"></a>
### B7

> ❓ **Question.** Two forces, $3\ \mathrm{N}$ east and $4\ \mathrm{N}$ north, act on a body. Find the net force.

**✅ Answer.** $F = \sqrt{3^2+4^2} = \boxed{5\ \mathrm{N}}$, directed $\tan^{-1}(4/3) \approx 53°$ north of east.

---

<a id="b8"></a>
### B8

> ❓ **Question.** A constant force gives a $3\ \mathrm{kg}$ body a velocity change from $2$ to $8\ \mathrm{m/s}$ in $2\ \mathrm{s}$. Find the force.

**✅ Answer.** $a = (8-2)/2 = 3\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$; $F = ma = 3(3) = \boxed{9\ \mathrm{N}}$.

---

<a id="b9"></a>
### B9

> ❓ **Question.** A stone of mass $0.5\ \mathrm{kg}$ is whirled in a horizontal circle of radius $1\ \mathrm{m}$ at $4\ \mathrm{m/s}$. Find the tension in the string.

**✅ Answer.** $T = \dfrac{mv^2}{r} = \dfrac{0.5(16)}{1} = \boxed{8\ \mathrm{N}}$.

---

<a id="b10"></a>
### B10

> ❓ **Question.** A body of mass $m$ is in equilibrium under three forces. Two are $6\ \mathrm{N}$ east and $8\ \mathrm{N}$ north. Find the third force.

**✅ Answer.** The third must cancel the resultant $\sqrt{6^2+8^2}=10\ \mathrm{N}$: so $\boxed{10\ \mathrm{N}}$ directed south-west ($\tan^{-1}(8/6)$ below west).

<a id="medium"></a>
## 🟡 Medium (M1–M15)

<a id="m1"></a>
### M1

> ❓ **Question.** A $4\ \mathrm{kg}$ block is pulled along a rough floor ($\mu_k = 0.25$) by a $20\ \mathrm{N}$ horizontal force. Find its acceleration.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Net force = applied − friction. Find $f_k = \mu_k mg$ first, subtract, divide by mass.

**Steps.** $f_k = 0.25(4)(10) = 10\ \mathrm{N}$; $F_{\text{net}} = 20-10 = 10\ \mathrm{N}$; $a = 10/4$.

**✅ Answer.** $\boxed{2.5\ \mathrm{m/s^2}}$.

---

<a id="m2"></a>
### M2

> ❓ **Question.** A $2\ \mathrm{kg}$ block slides down a frictionless incline of $30°$. Find its acceleration.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Only the along-slope component of gravity drives it: $a = g\sin\theta$ (mass cancels).

**Steps.** $a = g\sin30° = 10(0.5)$.

**✅ Answer.** $\boxed{5\ \mathrm{m/s^2}}$ down the incline.

---

<a id="m3"></a>
### M3

> ❓ **Question.** A $2\ \mathrm{kg}$ block slides down a $30°$ incline with $\mu_k = 0.2$. Find its acceleration.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Now subtract friction: $a = g(\sin\theta - \mu_k\cos\theta)$, with $N = mg\cos\theta$.

**Steps.** $a = 10(\sin30° - 0.2\cos30°) = 10(0.5 - 0.2\times0.866) = 10(0.5 - 0.173)$.

**✅ Answer.** $a \approx \boxed{3.27\ \mathrm{m/s^2}}$.

---

<a id="m4"></a>
### M4

> ❓ **Question.** In an Atwood machine, masses $3\ \mathrm{kg}$ and $5\ \mathrm{kg}$ hang over a frictionless pulley. Find the acceleration and the string tension.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** $a = \dfrac{(m_2 - m_1)g}{m_1 + m_2}$; then $T = m_1(g + a)$ (lighter mass rises).

**Steps.** $a = \dfrac{(5-3)(10)}{8} = 2.5\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$; $T = 3(10 + 2.5) = 37.5\ \mathrm{N}$.

**✅ Answer.** $a = 2.5\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$, $T = 37.5\ \mathrm{N}$.

---

<a id="m5"></a>
### M5

> ❓ **Question.** A block $m_1 = 4\ \mathrm{kg}$ on a frictionless table is connected over a pulley to a hanging block $m_2 = 6\ \mathrm{kg}$. Find the acceleration and tension.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** System: only $m_2 g$ drives the whole mass. $a = \dfrac{m_2 g}{m_1 + m_2}$; isolate $m_1$ for $T = m_1 a$.

**Steps.** $a = \dfrac{6(10)}{10} = 6\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$; $T = m_1 a = 4(6) = 24\ \mathrm{N}$.

**✅ Answer.** $a = 6\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$, $T = 24\ \mathrm{N}$.

---

<a id="m6"></a>
### M6

> ❓ **Question.** A person of mass $60\ \mathrm{kg}$ stands in a lift accelerating **upward** at $2\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find the normal force (apparent weight).

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** In the ground frame: $N - mg = ma \Rightarrow N = m(g+a)$ for upward acceleration.

**Steps.** $N = 60(10 + 2) = 60(12)$.

**✅ Answer.** $\boxed{720\ \mathrm{N}}$ (heavier than the $600\ \mathrm{N}$ true weight).

---

<a id="m7"></a>
### M7

> ❓ **Question.** The same lift ($m = 60\ \mathrm{kg}$) now accelerates **downward** at $2\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find the apparent weight. What if the cable snaps (free fall)?

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Downward acceleration: $N = m(g - a)$. Free fall means $a = g \Rightarrow N = 0$ (weightlessness).

**Steps.** $N = 60(10 - 2) = 480\ \mathrm{N}$; free fall: $N = 60(10-10) = 0$.

**✅ Answer.** $480\ \mathrm{N}$; in free fall $N = 0$ (apparent weightlessness).

---

<a id="m8"></a>
### M8

> ❓ **Question.** Two blocks, $2\ \mathrm{kg}$ and $3\ \mathrm{kg}$, are in contact on a frictionless floor. A $20\ \mathrm{N}$ force pushes the $2\ \mathrm{kg}$ block. Find the acceleration and the contact force between them.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Whole system: $a = F/(m_1+m_2)$. For the contact force, isolate the **second** block: $F_{\text{contact}} = m_2 a$.

**Steps.** $a = 20/5 = 4\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$; contact force $= 3(4) = 12\ \mathrm{N}$.

**✅ Answer.** $a = 4\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$, contact force $= 12\ \mathrm{N}$.

---

<a id="m9"></a>
### M9

> ❓ **Question.** A $1\ \mathrm{kg}$ block is held against a vertical wall by a horizontal force $F$. If $\mu_s = 0.5$, find the minimum $F$ to keep it from sliding.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** The wall's normal force is $N = F$ (horizontal). Friction (vertical) must hold the weight: $\mu_s F \ge mg$.

**Steps.** $\mu_s F = mg \Rightarrow F = \dfrac{mg}{\mu_s} = \dfrac{10}{0.5}$.

**✅ Answer.** $F_{\min} = \boxed{20\ \mathrm{N}}$.

---

<a id="m10"></a>
### M10

> ❓ **Question.** A car rounds a flat unbanked curve of radius $50\ \mathrm{m}$. If $\mu_s = 0.4$, find the maximum speed without skidding.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** On a flat road, **static friction** supplies the centripetal force: $\mu_s mg = \dfrac{mv^2}{r} \Rightarrow v_{\max} = \sqrt{\mu_s g r}$.

**Steps.** $v = \sqrt{0.4(10)(50)} = \sqrt{200}$.

**✅ Answer.** $v_{\max} \approx \boxed{14.1\ \mathrm{m/s}}$.

---

<a id="m11"></a>
### M11

> ❓ **Question.** A $0.2\ \mathrm{kg}$ ball on a string is whirled in a **vertical** circle of radius $0.5\ \mathrm{m}$. Find the minimum speed at the top to keep the string taut.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** At the top, the minimum case has $T=0$, so gravity alone is centripetal: $mg = \dfrac{mv^2}{r} \Rightarrow v = \sqrt{gr}$.

**Steps.** $v_{\text{top}} = \sqrt{10 \times 0.5} = \sqrt{5}$.

**✅ Answer.** $v_{\text{top}} \approx \boxed{2.24\ \mathrm{m/s}}$.

---

<a id="m12"></a>
### M12

> ❓ **Question.** A gun of mass $2\ \mathrm{kg}$ fires a $20\ \mathrm{g}$ bullet at $150\ \mathrm{m/s}$. Find the recoil velocity of the gun.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** No external horizontal force → momentum conserved: $m_g v_g = m_b v_b$ (opposite directions).

**Steps.** $v_g = \dfrac{m_b v_b}{m_g} = \dfrac{0.02(150)}{2} = \dfrac{3}{2}$.

**✅ Answer.** $v_g = 1.5\ \mathrm{m/s}$ backward.

---

<a id="m13"></a>
### M13

> ❓ **Question.** A block is projected up a rough $30°$ incline at $10\ \mathrm{m/s}$ ($\mu_k = 0.2$). Find its deceleration while going up.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Going **up**, both gravity and friction act down the slope: $a = g(\sin\theta + \mu_k\cos\theta)$.

**Steps.** $a = 10(0.5 + 0.2\times0.866) = 10(0.5 + 0.173)$.

**✅ Answer.** deceleration $\approx \boxed{6.73\ \mathrm{m/s^2}}$.

---

<a id="m14"></a>
### M14

> ❓ **Question.** A $70\ \mathrm{kg}$ man stands on a weighing scale in a lift. The scale reads $56\ \mathrm{kg\text{-}wt}$. Find the lift's acceleration and direction.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Scale reads $N$. $N = 56 \times 10 = 560\ \mathrm{N} < mg = 700\ \mathrm{N}$, so the lift accelerates **down**. $N = m(g-a)$.

**Steps.** $560 = 70(10 - a) \Rightarrow 10 - a = 8 \Rightarrow a = 2$.

**✅ Answer.** $a = 2\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$ **downward**.

---

<a id="m15"></a>
### M15

> ❓ **Question.** A $3\ \mathrm{kg}$ block on a $4\ \mathrm{kg}$ block (on a frictionless floor) is pulled by $14\ \mathrm{N}$ on the top block. The surface between them has $\mu_s = 0.5$. Do they move together? Find the common acceleration if so.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Assume they move together: $a = F/(m_1+m_2)$. Then check the friction needed on the **bottom** block ($f = m_2 a$) against $\mu_s N$ at the interface ($N = m_1 g$).

**Steps.** Assume together: $a = 14/7 = 2\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$. Friction needed on bottom block $= 4(2) = 8\ \mathrm{N}$. Max available $= \mu_s m_1 g = 0.5(3)(10) = 15\ \mathrm{N}$. Since $8 < 15$, they move together.

**✅ Answer.** Yes, they move together at $a = 2\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$.

<a id="advanced"></a>
## 🔴 Advanced (A1–A10)

<a id="a1"></a>
### A1

> ❓ **Question.** A pendulum bob hangs from the ceiling of a car accelerating horizontally at $5\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find the angle the string makes with the vertical.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** In the car's (non-inertial) frame add a pseudo-force $ma_0$ backward. Balance: $T\sin\theta = ma_0$, $T\cos\theta = mg \Rightarrow \tan\theta = a_0/g$.

**Steps.** $\tan\theta = 5/10 = 0.5 \Rightarrow \theta = \tan^{-1}(0.5)$.

**✅ Answer.** $\theta \approx \boxed{26.6°}$ from the vertical (toward the back).

---

<a id="a2"></a>
### A2

> ❓ **Question.** For a ball in a vertical circle of radius $r$ with the minimum speed to complete the loop, find the ratio of the string tension at the bottom to that at the top... wait, the top tension is zero. Instead find the tension at the bottom in terms of $mg$.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Minimum case: $v_{\text{top}} = \sqrt{gr}$; by energy conservation $v_{\text{bot}}^2 = v_{\text{top}}^2 + 2g(2r) = 5gr$. At the bottom $T - mg = mv_{\text{bot}}^2/r$.

**Steps.** $T = mg + \dfrac{m(5gr)}{r} = mg + 5mg = 6mg$.

**✅ Answer.** $T_{\text{bottom}} = \boxed{6mg}$.

---

<a id="a3"></a>
### A3

> ❓ **Question.** A curve of radius $100\ \mathrm{m}$ is banked at $\theta$ for a design speed of $20\ \mathrm{m/s}$ (no friction). Find $\theta$.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Frictionless banking: $\tan\theta = \dfrac{v^2}{rg}$ — the normal force's horizontal component supplies $F_c$.

**Steps.** $\tan\theta = \dfrac{400}{100(10)} = 0.4 \Rightarrow \theta = \tan^{-1}(0.4)$.

**✅ Answer.** $\theta \approx \boxed{21.8°}$.

---

<a id="a4"></a>
### A4

> ❓ **Question.** A block of mass $m$ rests on a wedge of incline $\theta$. The wedge is accelerated horizontally at $a$ so the block does **not** slide relative to the wedge (no friction). Find $a$.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** In the wedge frame, add pseudo-force $ma$ backward. For the block to stay put, the net of gravity + pseudo-force must be perpendicular to the incline: $\tan\theta = a/g$.

**Steps.** Along the incline: $mg\sin\theta = ma\cos\theta \Rightarrow a = g\tan\theta$.

**✅ Answer.** $a = g\tan\theta$.

---

<a id="a5"></a>
### A5

> ❓ **Question.** Sand falls onto a conveyor belt at a rate of $2\ \mathrm{kg/s}$. The belt moves at $4\ \mathrm{m/s}$. Find the extra force needed to keep the belt moving at constant speed.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Variable-mass: $F = \dfrac{dp}{dt} = v\dfrac{dm}{dt}$ (belt speed constant, mass being added gains momentum).

**Steps.** $F = v\dfrac{dm}{dt} = 4(2)$.

**✅ Answer.** $F = \boxed{8\ \mathrm{N}}$.

---

<a id="a6"></a>
### A6

> ❓ **Question.** A block of mass $m_1 = 2\ \mathrm{kg}$ sits on top of $m_2 = 4\ \mathrm{kg}$ on a frictionless floor. The coefficient of friction between the blocks is $\mu_s = 0.3$. Find the maximum horizontal force applied to the **bottom** block so they still move together.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Moving together, $a = F/(m_1+m_2)$. The friction on the **top** block provides its acceleration; max is $\mu_s m_1 g$. Set $m_1 a_{\max} = \mu_s m_1 g \Rightarrow a_{\max} = \mu_s g$, then $F_{\max} = (m_1+m_2)a_{\max}$.

**Steps.** $a_{\max} = 0.3(10) = 3\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$; $F_{\max} = 6(3) = 18\ \mathrm{N}$.

**✅ Answer.** $F_{\max} = \boxed{18\ \mathrm{N}}$.

---

<a id="a7"></a>
### A7

> ❓ **Question.** In a vertical circle of radius $2\ \mathrm{m}$, a $1\ \mathrm{kg}$ ball has speed $10\ \mathrm{m/s}$ at the bottom. Find its speed and the string tension at the top.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Energy conservation for the speed: $v_{\text{top}}^2 = v_{\text{bot}}^2 - 2g(2r)$. Then at the top $T + mg = mv_{\text{top}}^2/r$.

**Steps.** $v_{\text{top}}^2 = 100 - 2(10)(4) = 20 \Rightarrow v_{\text{top}} = \sqrt{20} \approx 4.47\ \mathrm{m/s}$. $T = \dfrac{1(20)}{2} - 1(10) = 10 - 10 = 0$.

**✅ Answer.** $v_{\text{top}} \approx 4.47\ \mathrm{m/s}$, $T = 0$ (exactly the minimum-loop condition, since $v_{\text{bot}}=\sqrt{5gr}=\sqrt{100}=10$).

---

<a id="a8"></a>
### A8

> ❓ **Question.** Three blocks of masses $1, 2, 3\ \mathrm{kg}$ are connected by strings and pulled along a frictionless floor by a $12\ \mathrm{N}$ force applied to the $3\ \mathrm{kg}$ block (order: $1$–$2$–$3$, force on 3). Find the tension in the string between the $1\ \mathrm{kg}$ and $2\ \mathrm{kg}$ blocks.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** System acceleration $a = F/\sum m$. The string between $1$ and $2$ pulls only the $1\ \mathrm{kg}$ block, so $T_{12} = m_1 a$.

**Steps.** $a = 12/6 = 2\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$; $T_{12} = 1(2)$.

**✅ Answer.** $T_{12} = \boxed{2\ \mathrm{N}}$.

---

<a id="a9"></a>
### A9

> ❓ **Question.** A chain of length $L$ lies on a rough table with a fraction hanging over the edge. If $\mu_s = 0.25$, find the maximum fraction that can hang without the chain sliding.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Let fraction $x$ hang. Weight of hanging part pulls; friction on the part *on* the table resists. At the verge: $(x)(L\lambda g) = \mu_s (1-x)(L\lambda g)$, where $\lambda$ is mass per length.

**Steps.** $x = \mu_s(1-x) \Rightarrow x(1+\mu_s) = \mu_s \Rightarrow x = \dfrac{\mu_s}{1+\mu_s} = \dfrac{0.25}{1.25}$.

**✅ Answer.** $x = 0.2$, i.e. **1/5 (20%)** of the chain may hang.

---

<a id="a10"></a>
### A10

> ❓ **Question.** A block of mass $M$ on a frictionless incline (angle $\theta$) is connected over a pulley at the top to a hanging mass $m$. Find the acceleration of the system (take $m > M\sin\theta$).

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Two FBDs. Hanging mass: $mg - T = ma$. Incline block (moving up the slope): $T - Mg\sin\theta = Ma$. Add to eliminate $T$.

**Steps.** $mg - Mg\sin\theta = (m+M)a \Rightarrow a = \dfrac{(m - M\sin\theta)g}{m+M}$.

**✅ Answer.** $a = \dfrac{(m - M\sin\theta)\,g}{m+M}$ (hanging mass descends).
