## 🎯 Quick summary (the whole topic in a nutshell)

The Laws of Motion explain **why** motion changes — the cause is **force**. Everything in this chapter
grows out of Newton's three laws, applied through one habit: **draw a free-body diagram.**

**The seven things to really understand:**

1. **Newton's three laws.** 1st: no net force → constant velocity (defines inertia & inertial frames).
   2nd: $\vec F = d\vec p/dt = m\vec a$. 3rd: action and reaction are equal, opposite, and on
   **different** bodies (so they never cancel).
2. **Free-body diagrams.** Isolate one body, draw every force on it (weight, normal, tension, friction,
   applied), resolve along smart axes, write $\sum F = ma$. The normal force is a variable — solve for it.
3. **Friction.** Static friction self-adjusts up to $\mu_s N$; kinetic is $\mu_k N$. Check whether the
   body actually slides before using $\mu_s N$.
4. **Inclines.** Split gravity into $mg\sin\theta$ (down slope) and $mg\cos\theta$ (into slope). Stays if
   $\tan\theta \le \mu_s$.
5. **Connected bodies.** Strings over ideal pulleys share one acceleration and equal tension. System
   method for $a$, then isolate a body for the tension.
6. **Circular motion.** A net **inward** force $mv^2/r$ is required, supplied by real forces (tension,
   gravity, friction, normal). "Centripetal force" is a name, not a new force.
7. **Lift / apparent weight.** $N = m(g \pm a)$; zero in free fall.

**The master trick (advanced):** for a body **at rest in an accelerating frame** (bob in a car, block on
a wedge), switch to that frame and add the pseudo-force $-m\vec a_0$ — the body is then in equilibrium and
the algebra collapses.
