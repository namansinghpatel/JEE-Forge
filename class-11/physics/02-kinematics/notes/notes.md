## 🎯 Quick summary (the whole topic in a nutshell)

Kinematics is the study of **how things move** — position, velocity, acceleration — without worrying
about the forces causing it. It splits into rectilinear (straight-line) motion, projectile motion,
and relative velocity.

**The six things to really understand:**

1. **Distance vs displacement, speed vs velocity.** Distance and speed are scalars (path-based,
   never negative); displacement and velocity are vectors (straight-line, can be negative or zero).
2. **The three equations of motion** ($v=u+at$, $s=ut+\tfrac12at^2$, $v^2=u^2+2as$) work **only when
   acceleration is constant.** Pick a positive direction and keep signs consistent.
3. **When acceleration varies, go back to calculus:** $a = dv/dt$ (if $a$ depends on time) and
   $a = v\,dv/dx$ (if $a$ depends on position). This second one is the easily-forgotten key.
4. **Projectiles = two independent motions.** Horizontal is constant velocity; vertical is free
   fall. Range is biggest at $45°$, complementary angles give the same range, and the path is a
   parabola.
5. **Relative velocity is vector subtraction:** $\vec v_{AB} = \vec v_A - \vec v_B$. River-boat and
   rain-man problems are all this one idea drawn as a vector triangle.
6. **Graphs are shortcuts:** slope of $x$–$t$ is velocity, slope of $v$–$t$ is acceleration, and the
   **area** under $v$–$t$ is displacement.

**The master trick (advanced):** for two moving bodies, **sit in one body's frame** — the other then
moves in a straight line, and closest-approach / collision problems become simple geometry.
