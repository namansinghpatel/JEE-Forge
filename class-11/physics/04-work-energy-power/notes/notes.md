## 🎯 Quick summary (the whole topic in a nutshell)

This chapter gives you **energy** as a second, often faster, way to solve mechanics — you trade force
diagrams for a single scalar bookkeeping equation.

**The seven things to really understand:**

1. **Work** $W = \vec F\cdot\vec d = Fd\cos\theta$. Zero when the force is perpendicular to motion;
   negative when it opposes motion (friction, gravity on the way up). For a varying force, work is the
   **area under the $F$–$x$ graph**.
2. **Work–energy theorem:** net work = change in kinetic energy, $W_{\text{net}} = \tfrac12 mv^2 - \tfrac12 mu^2$.
   Links force·distance to speed **without time**.
3. **Potential energy:** gravitational $mgh$, spring $\tfrac12 kx^2$. Defined only for conservative forces.
4. **Conservation of mechanical energy:** if only conservative forces act, $K + U$ is constant. Friction
   shows up as a **loss** equal to $f \times$ path length.
5. **Power** = rate of work, $P = W/t = Fv$. A car's top speed is set by power ÷ resistance.
6. **Collisions:** momentum is **always** conserved; kinetic energy is conserved only if the collision is
   **elastic** ($e = 1$). Start every collision with momentum, add restitution as the second equation.
7. **$F = -dU/dx$:** the force is the negative slope of the PE curve; a minimum of $U$ is stable
   equilibrium (and behaves like a spring → SHM later).

**The master trick (advanced):** when speed changes on a track, spring, or loop, use **energy
conservation first** to get the speed, then apply the force equation ($mv^2/r$, Hooke's law) only where a
force or tension is actually asked.
