## 🎯 Quick summary (the whole topic in a nutshell)

This chapter is about the **periodic table as a prediction machine**: once you know an element's position,
you can predict its properties — no memorising needed.

**The seven things to really understand:**

1. **Modern periodic law:** properties repeat periodically with **atomic number** (not mass — Moseley's fix
   of Mendeleev).
2. **Periods & groups:** period = valence shell $n$; group = valence configuration. The table splits into
   **s, p, d, f blocks** by the last electron's subshell.
3. **Atomic/ionic radius:** decreases across a period, increases down a group. Cations < atom < anions; for
   isoelectronic species, more protons → smaller.
4. **Ionisation enthalpy:** increases across, decreases down — with dips at B<Be, Al<Mg (filled $s^2$) and
   O<N, S<P (half-filled $p^3$).
5. **Electron gain enthalpy:** more negative across, less negative down; halogens most negative — but **Cl >
   F** because F is so small.
6. **Electronegativity:** increases across, decreases down; **F is the maximum**. Metallic character and
   oxide basicity run the opposite way.
7. **The master variable is $Z_{\text{eff}}$** (effective nuclear charge = $Z - \sigma$): smaller atom +
   higher $Z_{\text{eff}}$ → holds and attracts electrons more strongly.

**The master trick (advanced):** a **large jump in successive ionisation enthalpies** reveals the group —
count the electrons removed before the jump. And "read position → predict chemistry" (block, oxidation
state, oxide nature) is the core inorganic skill.
