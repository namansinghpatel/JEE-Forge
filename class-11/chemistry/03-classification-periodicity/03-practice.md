# Classification & Periodicity — Practice Range

> **35 problems to solve yourself**, split **Basic (10) · Medium (15) · Advanced (10)**. Mostly
> reasoning — *arrange, compare, predict, identify* — with a few numericals ($Z_{\text{eff}}$, position,
> successive IE). Each has a **💡 Approach hint** (reveal only if stuck) and a **collapsible answer**.

<!-- RENDERING: inside math use \cdot (not ·), \to for arrows, Z_eff, superscripts for configs. -->

> **Guiding idea:** smaller atom + higher $Z_{\text{eff}}$ → higher IE, more negative electron gain enthalpy, higher electronegativity, more non-metallic/acidic.

## 🟡 Medium (P11–P25)

**P11.** Arrange by increasing radius: F, O, N, C.

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

Same period; radius decreases left to right.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

F < O < N < C.
</details>

---

**P12.** Arrange the isoelectronic ions by increasing radius: Al³⁺, Mg²⁺, Na⁺, F⁻.

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

More protons → smaller.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

Al³⁺ < Mg²⁺ < Na⁺ < F⁻.
</details>

---

**P13.** Why is IE of B less than that of Be?

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

$2p^1$ vs stable $2s^2$.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

B loses a loosely-held $2p$ electron; Be has a stable filled $2s^2$.
</details>

---

**P14.** Arrange by increasing first IE: Al, Mg, Si, Na.

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

Trend Na<Mg<Al<Si, but Al<Mg.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

Na < Al < Mg < Si.
</details>

---

**P15.** Which has the more negative electron gain enthalpy, O or S? Why?

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

Small size of O → repulsion.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

**S** — O's compact $2p$ shell causes extra electron–electron repulsion.
</details>

**P16.** Identify: period 2, group 16.

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

$2s^2 2p^4$.

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

**Oxygen.**
</details>

**P20.** Predict the nature of the oxides: MgO, Al₂O₃, P₄O₁₀.

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

**P21.** Successive IEs (kJ/mol): 496, 4562, 6912, 9544. Identify the group.

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

Jump after the 1st IE.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

**Group 1** (sodium).
</details>

---

**P22.** Which has the higher 2nd ionisation enthalpy: Mg or Al? Why?

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

After losing one electron, is removing the next one from a stable arrangement or toward one?
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

**Al.** Mg⁺ is $[\text{Ne}]3s^1$, so its 2nd electron removal *gives* the stable $[\text{Ne}]$ core (easier → lower IE). Al⁺ is $[\text{Ne}]3s^2$, so its 2nd electron comes from a filled $3s^2$ (harder → higher IE). So 2nd IE: Al > Mg.
</details>

</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

Most: **Ga**; least: **C**.
</details>

**P24.** Which element is most electronegative: N, O, F, Cl?

</details>

**P25.** Predict the maximum oxidation state of a group-15 element.

## 🔴 Advanced (P26–P35)

**P26.** Using Slater's rules, estimate $Z_{\text{eff}}$ for a $2p$ electron in nitrogen ($Z=7$, $1s^2 2s^2 2p^3$).

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

Same group ($2s,2p$) 4 others ×0.35; $1s$ 2 ×0.85.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

$\sigma = 4(0.35) + 2(0.85) = 1.4 + 1.7 = 3.1$; $Z_{\text{eff}} = 7 - 3.1 = \mathbf{3.9}$.
</details>

---

**P27.** Successive IEs (kJ/mol): 737, 1451, 7733, 10543, 13630. Which group?

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

Jump after the 2nd.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

**Group 2** (magnesium).
</details>

---

**P28.** Give a shared property showing the Be–Al diagonal relationship.

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

Both amphoteric.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

Both form **amphoteric oxides** (BeO, Al₂O₃) and covalent, polymeric chlorides.
</details>

---

**P29.** Arrange by increasing size: N³⁻, O²⁻, F⁻, Ne, Na⁺.

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

Isoelectronic (10 e⁻); fewer protons → larger.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

Na⁺ < Ne < F⁻ < O²⁻ < N³⁻.
</details>

---

**P30.** Why does fluorine, not chlorine, have the highest electronegativity despite Cl's more negative electron gain enthalpy?

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

Electronegativity is about attraction within a bond; F is smallest.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

F's very small size gives the highest charge density and pull on bonding electrons; electron gain enthalpy (an isolated-atom property) is reduced by F's compact-shell repulsion.
</details>

---

**P31.** Predict: an element forms a basic oxide and has one valence electron. Likely block and group?

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

Basic oxide + 1 valence e⁻.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

**s-block, group 1** (alkali metal).
</details>

---

**P32.** Which has higher first IE: N or O? And why does this invert the smooth trend?

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

Half-filled $2p^3$.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

**N** (stable half-filled $2p^3$), so IE(N) > IE(O) against the left-to-right rise.
</details>

---

**P33.** The oxide X₂O is basic and X has a very low first IE. Within period 3, name X.

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

X₂O stoichiometry → +1; lowest IE in period 3.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

**Sodium (Na).**
</details>

---

**P34.** Estimate $Z_{\text{eff}}$ for a $3s$ electron in sodium ($Z=11$, $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^1$) via Slater.

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

Same group: 0 others; $n=2$ (8) ×0.85; $n=1$ (2) ×1.0.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

$\sigma = 0 + 8(0.85) + 2(1.0) = 6.8 + 2 = 8.8$; $Z_{\text{eff}} = 11 - 8.8 = \mathbf{2.2}$.
</details>

---

**P35.** Arrange by increasing first ionisation enthalpy: Be, B, C, N, O.

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

Trend rises, with B<Be and O<N dips.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

B < Be < C < O < N.
</details>
