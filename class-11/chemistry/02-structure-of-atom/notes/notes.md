## 🎯 Quick summary (the whole topic in a nutshell)

This chapter is about **where electrons are and how much energy they have** inside an atom — the
foundation for the whole of chemistry (periodic table, bonding, reactivity).

**The seven things to really understand:**

1. **Subatomic particles & isotopes.** $Z$ = protons (defines the element), $A$ = protons + neutrons.
   Isotopes (same $Z$), isobars (same $A$), isoelectronic (same electrons).
2. **Light is quantised energy.** $E = h\nu = hc/\lambda$. Atoms emit/absorb only certain wavelengths
   (line spectra) → electron energies are **quantised**.
3. **Bohr's model** (one-electron atoms only): $E_n = -13.6\,Z^2/n^2$ eV, $r_n = 0.529\,n^2/Z$ Å. Energy
   is negative and rises toward zero as $n\to\infty$ (ionisation).
4. **Dual nature.** Light is wave + particle; so is matter: $\lambda = h/mv$ (de Broglie). The
   photoelectric effect depends on **frequency, not brightness**.
5. **Uncertainty principle.** $\Delta x\,\Delta p \ge h/4\pi$ — we can't pin down both position and
   momentum, so electrons live in **orbitals** (probability clouds), not fixed paths.
6. **Four quantum numbers** $(n, l, m_l, m_s)$ are each electron's unique address. Nodes: total $= n-1$.
7. **Electronic configuration** follows **Aufbau, Pauli, Hund**, with Cr and Cu as half/fully-filled
   exceptions. For ions, **remove 4s before 3d**.

**The master trick (advanced):** for any hydrogen-like ion (He⁺, Li²⁺) just **scale the hydrogen numbers**
by $Z^2/n^2$ (energy), $1/Z$ (radius), $Z$ (velocity) — no need to redo the derivation.
