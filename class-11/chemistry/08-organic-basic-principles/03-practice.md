# Organic Chemistry (GOC) — Practice Range

> **35 problems to solve yourself**, split **Basic (10) · Medium (15) · Advanced (10)**. Mostly reasoning —
> *name, classify, rank, predict* — with a few counting problems. Each has a **💡 Approach hint** (reveal
> only if stuck) and a **collapsible answer**. Attempt with pen and paper first.

<!-- RENDERING: inside math use \cdot (not ·), \to, \delta, \text{} for groups. -->

> **Guiding idea:** "what stabilises the key species?" — carbocation, radical, conjugate base, conjugate acid. Electronic effects are the tools.

## 🟢 Basic (P1–P10)

**P1.** Hybridisation of carbon in methane?

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

4 σ bonds, tetrahedral.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

$sp^3$.
</details>

---

**P2.** Classify $\text{CH}_3^-$.

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

Negative carbon with a lone pair.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

**Carbanion.**
</details>

---

**P3.** Is $\text{H}^+$ an electrophile or nucleophile?

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

Electron-deficient.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

**Electrophile.**
</details>

---

**P4.** IUPAC name of $\text{CH}_3\text{COCH}_3$?

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

Ketone, 3 carbons.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

Propan-2-one (acetone).
</details>

---

**P5.** Fission giving ions is called?

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

One atom keeps both electrons.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

**Heterolytic.**
</details>

---

**P6.** Bond angle in an $sp^2$ carbon?

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

Trigonal planar.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

$120°$.
</details>

---

**P7.** Which group is +M (electron-donating by resonance): $\text{–NO}_2$ or $\text{–OH}$?

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

Lone pair to donate?
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

$\text{–OH}$.
</details>

---

**P8.** σ and π bonds in ethyne?

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

Triple bond = 1σ + 2π.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

3 σ (2 C–H + 1 C–C) + 2 π.
</details>

---

**P9.** Most stable carbocation: 1°, 2°, or 3°?

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

More alkyl groups.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

$3°$.
</details>

---

**P10.** IUPAC name of $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{COOH}$?

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

–COOH, 3 carbons.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

Propanoic acid.
</details>

## 🟡 Medium (P11–P25)

**P11.** IUPAC name of $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{CCH}_2\text{OH}$.

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

Longest chain with –OH; name branches.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

2,2-dimethylpropan-1-ol.
</details>

---

**P12.** Degree of unsaturation of $\text{C}_6\text{H}_{10}$.

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

$(2C+2-H)/2$.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

$(14-10)/2 = \mathbf{2}$.
</details>

---

**P13.** How many structural isomers for $\text{C}_5\text{H}_{12}$?

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

Distinct carbon skeletons.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

$\mathbf{3}$ (n-pentane, isopentane, neopentane).
</details>

---

**P14.** More acidic: acetic acid or formic acid?

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

CH₃ +I weakens acidity.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

**Formic acid.**
</details>

---

**P15.** Identify isomerism: but-1-ene and but-2-ene.

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

Same group, different position.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

**Position** isomerism.
</details>

---

**P16.** α-hydrogens stabilising the isopropyl cation $(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CH}^+$?

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

Count C–H on adjacent carbons.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

$2\times3 = \mathbf{6}$.
</details>

---

**P17.** Which shows geometrical isomerism: 2-methylbut-2-ene or but-2-ene?

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

Need two different groups on each sp² carbon.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

**But-2-ene** (2-methylbut-2-ene has two identical methyls on one carbon).
</details>

---

**P18.** Rank carbocation stability: benzyl, methyl, ethyl.

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

Resonance beats hyperconjugation.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

benzyl > ethyl > methyl.
</details>

---

**P19.** IUPAC name of $\text{CH}_2\text{=CHCH}_2\text{CH}_3$.

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

Double bond lowest locant.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

But-1-ene.
</details>

---

**P20.** Which is more basic: aniline or methylamine?

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

Aniline's lone pair delocalised into ring.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

**Methylamine.**
</details>

---

**P21.** Degree of unsaturation of $\text{C}_4\text{H}_6$.

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

$(2\cdot4+2-6)/2$.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

$\mathbf{2}$ (e.g. one triple bond or two double bonds/ring).
</details>

---

**P22.** Identify the isomerism: propan-1-ol and propan-2-ol.

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

Same group, different carbon.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

**Position** isomerism.
</details>

---

**P23.** More acidic: phenol or ethanol?

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

Resonance stabilisation of conjugate base.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

**Phenol** (phenoxide is resonance-stabilised).
</details>

---

**P24.** Name the effect: delocalisation of σ(C–H) electrons into an adjacent empty p-orbital.

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

"No-bond resonance."
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

**Hyperconjugation.**
</details>

---

**P25.** Number of isomeric ethers of $\text{C}_4\text{H}_{10}\text{O}$ (ethers only).

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

Split the oxygen between two alkyl groups.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

$\mathbf{3}$ (methyl propyl ether, methyl isopropyl ether, diethyl ether).
</details>

<a id="advanced"></a>
## 🔴 Advanced (P26–P35)

**P26.** Is the cycloheptatrienyl (tropylium) cation $\text{C}_7\text{H}_7^+$ aromatic?

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

Count π electrons; $4n+2$?
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

6 π electrons → **aromatic**.
</details>

---

**P27.** Adding HBr to but-1-ene: give the major product and the governing rule.

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

Markovnikov → more stable carbocation.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

2-bromobutane (Markovnikov; 2° cation more stable).
</details>

---

**P28.** Rank acidity: p-nitrophenol, phenol, p-cresol (p-methylphenol).

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

–NO₂ (–I/–M) increases, –CH₃ (+I) decreases acidity.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

p-nitrophenol > phenol > p-cresol.
</details>

---

**P29.** Is cyclooctatetraene ($\text{C}_8\text{H}_8$) aromatic? (It is non-planar/tub-shaped.)

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

Aromaticity needs planarity.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

**Non-aromatic** (non-planar, so not fully conjugated in a planar ring).
</details>

---

**P30.** Which undergoes $S_N1$ faster: 2-bromopropane or 2-bromo-2-methylpropane?

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

Carbocation stability.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

**2-bromo-2-methylpropane** (3° carbocation).
</details>

---

**P31.** On dehydration of 3,3-dimethylbutan-2-ol, a rearranged alkene forms. Why?

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

2° cation → 1,2-methyl shift → 3° cation.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

The 2° carbocation rearranges by a 1,2-methyl shift to a more stable 3° cation, giving 2,3-dimethylbut-2-ene as the major product.
</details>

---

**P32.** Rank basicity (gas phase): ammonia, methylamine, dimethylamine, trimethylamine.

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

Gas phase → +I only.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

(CH₃)₃N > (CH₃)₂NH > CH₃NH₂ > NH₃.
</details>

---

**P33.** How many π electrons make furan aromatic, and where does the extra lone pair come from?

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

Oxygen lone pair in the ring p-system.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

6 π electrons (4 from two C=C + 2 from one O lone pair) → aromatic.
</details>

---

**P34.** Rank acidity: ethyne, ethene, ethane.

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

More s-character → more acidic C–H.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

ethyne (sp) > ethene (sp²) > ethane (sp³).
</details>

---

**P35.** Why does chlorobenzene direct electrophiles ortho/para despite Cl being electron-withdrawing (–I)?

<details><summary>💡 Approach hint</summary>

+M (lone-pair donation) vs –I; position vs rate.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer</summary>

Cl's +M (lone-pair resonance into the ring) directs **o/p**, while its stronger –I **deactivates** the ring — so it's an o/p-directing deactivator.
</details>
