# Organic Chemistry (GOC) — Solved Problems

> **35 worked problems**, split **Basic (10) · Medium (15) · Advanced (10)**. GOC is a **reasoning**
> chapter, so most problems ask you to *name, classify, rank or predict* — with a few counting problems
> (degree of unsaturation, isomers, α-hydrogens). Every problem marks the **❓ Question** and **✅ Answer**;
> Medium and Advanced carry a **🧭 Strategy** block.

<!-- RENDERING: inside math use \cdot (not ·), \to, \delta, \text{} for groups. -->

> **Guiding idea:** almost every answer reduces to *"what stabilises the key species?"* — carbocation, radical, conjugate base, or conjugate acid. Electronic effects (inductive, resonance, hyperconjugation) are the tools.

## 🟢 Basic (B1–B10)

<a id="b1"></a>
### B1

> ❓ **Question.** What is the hybridisation of carbon in ethyne ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_2$)?

**✅ Answer.** $sp$ (linear, $180°$; one σ + two π between the carbons).

---

<a id="b2"></a>
### B2

> ❓ **Question.** Classify the intermediate $\text{CH}_3^+$.

**✅ Answer.** A **carbocation** (electron-deficient, $sp^2$, trigonal planar).

---

<a id="b3"></a>
### B3

> ❓ **Question.** Is $\text{OH}^-$ a nucleophile or an electrophile?

**✅ Answer.** A **nucleophile** (electron-pair donor).

---

<a id="b4"></a>
### B4

> ❓ **Question.** Give the IUPAC name of $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$.

**✅ Answer.** Propan-1-ol.

---

<a id="b5"></a>
### B5

> ❓ **Question.** Name the type of fission that gives free radicals.

**✅ Answer.** **Homolytic** fission.

---

<a id="b6"></a>
### B6

> ❓ **Question.** Which has more s-character: $sp^2$ or $sp^3$ carbon?

**✅ Answer.** $sp^2$ ($33\%$ vs $25\%$).

---

<a id="b7"></a>
### B7

> ❓ **Question.** Name the principal functional group suffix in CH₃COOH.

**✅ Answer.** –oic acid (ethanoic acid); –COOH is highest priority.

---

<a id="b8"></a>
### B8

> ❓ **Question.** Is $\text{NO}_2$ a +I or –I group?

**✅ Answer.** **–I** (electron-withdrawing).

---

<a id="b9"></a>
### B9

> ❓ **Question.** State whether an alkyl group is electron-donating or withdrawing (inductive).

**✅ Answer.** Electron-**donating** (+I).

---

<a id="b10"></a>
### B10

> ❓ **Question.** How many σ and π bonds are in ethene ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_4$)?

**✅ Answer.** 5 σ (4 C–H + 1 C–C) and 1 π.

<a id="medium"></a>
## 🟡 Medium (M1–M15)

<a id="m1"></a>
### M1

> ❓ **Question.** Give the IUPAC name of $(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CHCH}_2\text{CH}_3$.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Longest chain is 4 carbons with a methyl branch.

**Steps.** Longest chain: butane; methyl at C2.

**✅ Answer.** 2-methylbutane.

---

<a id="m2"></a>
### M2

> ❓ **Question.** Rank the carbocations by stability: $\text{CH}_3^+$, $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2^+$, $(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CH}^+$, $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C}^+$.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** More alkyl groups → more +I and hyperconjugation → more stable.

**Steps.** $3° > 2° > 1° > \text{methyl}$.

**✅ Answer.** $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C}^+ > (\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CH}^+ > \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2^+ > \text{CH}_3^+$.

---

<a id="m3"></a>
### M3

> ❓ **Question.** Compute the degree of unsaturation of $\text{C}_5\text{H}_8$.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Degree of unsaturation $= \dfrac{2C + 2 + N - H - X}{2}$.

**Steps.** $= \dfrac{2(5) + 2 - 8}{2} = \dfrac{4}{2}$.

**✅ Answer.** $2$ (e.g. one ring + one double bond, or one triple bond).

---

<a id="m4"></a>
### M4

> ❓ **Question.** How many structural isomers does $\text{C}_4\text{H}_{10}$ have?

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Draw distinct carbon skeletons.

**Steps.** n-butane and isobutane (2-methylpropane).

**✅ Answer.** $2$.

---

<a id="m5"></a>
### M5

> ❓ **Question.** Which is more acidic: ethanoic acid or chloroethanoic acid? Why?

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** –I of Cl stabilises the conjugate base → stronger acid.

**Steps.** Cl's –I withdraws electron density, stabilising the carboxylate.

**✅ Answer.** **Chloroethanoic acid** (stronger acid, due to the –I of chlorine).

---

<a id="m6"></a>
### M6

> ❓ **Question.** Identify the isomerism between ethanol and dimethyl ether.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Same formula ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_6\text{O}$), different functional group.

**Steps.** –OH (alcohol) vs –O– (ether).

**✅ Answer.** **Functional** isomerism.

---

<a id="m7"></a>
### M7

> ❓ **Question.** How many α-hydrogens stabilise the tert-butyl cation $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C}^+$ by hyperconjugation?

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Count C–H bonds on carbons adjacent to the positive carbon.

**Steps.** Three CH₃ groups → $3\times3 = 9$.

**✅ Answer.** $9$ α-hydrogens.

---

<a id="m8"></a>
### M8

> ❓ **Question.** Which carbon shows geometrical isomerism: $\text{CH}_3\text{CH=CHCH}_3$ or $\text{CH}_2\text{=CH}_2$? Why?

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Need two different groups on each doubly-bonded carbon.

**Steps.** But-2-ene has CH₃ and H on each sp² carbon → cis/trans; ethene has two identical H's.

**✅ Answer.** **But-2-ene** (CH₃CH=CHCH₃) shows cis–trans isomerism.

---

<a id="m9"></a>
### M9

> ❓ **Question.** Is $\text{NO}_2^+$ an electrophile or a nucleophile, and in what reaction is it the attacking species?

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Positive, electron-deficient → electrophile.

**Steps.** Nitronium ion attacks electron-rich aromatic rings.

**✅ Answer.** **Electrophile** (in aromatic nitration).

---

<a id="m10"></a>
### M10

> ❓ **Question.** Name $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{CHO}$ by IUPAC.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** –CHO = aldehyde (suffix –al), C1 is the carbonyl carbon.

**Steps.** 4-carbon chain ending in CHO.

**✅ Answer.** Butanal.

---

<a id="m11"></a>
### M11

> ❓ **Question.** Rank acidity: ethanol, water, phenol.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Resonance stabilisation of the conjugate base vs +I destabilisation.

**Steps.** Phenoxide is resonance-stabilised; alkoxide is destabilised by +I of ethyl.

**✅ Answer.** phenol > water > ethanol.

---

<a id="m12"></a>
### M12

> ❓ **Question.** Degree of unsaturation of benzene ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_6$)?

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** $\dfrac{2(6)+2-6}{2}$.

**Steps.** $= \dfrac{8}{2} = 4$ (3 double bonds + 1 ring).

**✅ Answer.** $4$.

---

<a id="m13"></a>
### M13

> ❓ **Question.** Which is more stable, the allyl cation $\text{CH}_2\text{=CH-CH}_2^+$ or the ethyl cation? Why?

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Resonance delocalisation beats hyperconjugation.

**Steps.** Allyl cation's positive charge is delocalised over two carbons by resonance.

**✅ Answer.** **Allyl cation** (resonance-stabilised).

---

<a id="m14"></a>
### M14

> ❓ **Question.** Give the number of isomeric alcohols of formula $\text{C}_4\text{H}_{10}\text{O}$ (alcohols only).

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Place –OH on the distinct butyl skeletons.

**Steps.** butan-1-ol, butan-2-ol, 2-methylpropan-1-ol, 2-methylpropan-2-ol.

**✅ Answer.** $4$ alcohols.

---

<a id="m15"></a>
### M15

> ❓ **Question.** Which is more basic: ammonia or methylamine? Why?

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** +I of the methyl group increases electron density on N.

**Steps.** CH₃ donates electron density, making the lone pair more available.

**✅ Answer.** **Methylamine** (more basic than ammonia).

<a id="advanced"></a>
## 🔴 Advanced (A1–A10)

<a id="a1"></a>
### A1

> ❓ **Question.** Is the cyclopentadienyl anion ($\text{C}_5\text{H}_5^-$) aromatic? Justify with Hückel's rule.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Cyclic, planar, fully conjugated? Count π electrons for $4n+2$.

**Steps.** 5-membered ring, planar, conjugated; 2 (per double bond ×2) + 2 (lone pair on the carbanion) = 6 π electrons = $4(1)+2$.

**✅ Answer.** **Yes, aromatic** (6 π electrons, $n=1$).

---

<a id="a2"></a>
### A2

> ❓ **Question.** On adding HBr to 3-methylbut-1-ene, a rearranged product dominates. Explain.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Markovnikov gives a 2° cation, which undergoes a 1,2-hydride shift to a more stable 3° cation.

**Steps.** Protonation gives a secondary carbocation at C2; a hydride shifts from C3 to give the tertiary cation; Br⁻ attacks there.

**✅ Answer.** The 2° carbocation rearranges (1,2-hydride shift) to a 3° carbocation, so the major product is 2-bromo-2-methylbutane.

---

<a id="a3"></a>
### A3

> ❓ **Question.** Rank acidity: acetic acid, formic acid, trichloroacetic acid.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** –I groups near –COOH stabilise the carboxylate.

**Steps.** Three Cl's (strong –I) ≫ formic (no alkyl +I) > acetic (CH₃ +I weakens).

**✅ Answer.** trichloroacetic > formic > acetic acid.

---

<a id="a4"></a>
### A4

> ❓ **Question.** Is cyclobutadiene ($\text{C}_4\text{H}_4$) aromatic, antiaromatic or non-aromatic?

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Cyclic, planar, conjugated, 4 π electrons → $4n$.

**Steps.** 4 π electrons ($n=1$) → antiaromatic (destabilised).

**✅ Answer.** **Antiaromatic.**

---

<a id="a5"></a>
### A5

> ❓ **Question.** Compare basicity in the gas phase: $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{N}$ vs $\text{CH}_3\text{NH}_2$.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** In the gas phase (no solvation), basicity follows +I only.

**Steps.** More alkyl groups → more +I → more available lone pair.

**✅ Answer.** $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{N} > \text{CH}_3\text{NH}_2$ in the **gas phase** (solvation reverses parts of this order in water).

---

<a id="a6"></a>
### A6

> ❓ **Question.** Why is aniline a weaker base than ammonia?

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** The N lone pair is delocalised into the benzene ring (resonance).

**Steps.** Conjugation into the ring reduces lone-pair availability on N.

**✅ Answer.** Aniline's N lone pair is delocalised into the ring (resonance), so it is **less available** → weaker base than ammonia.

---

<a id="a7"></a>
### A7

> ❓ **Question.** Identify the most stable resonance contributor for the carboxylate ion $\text{RCOO}^-$ and state why the two C–O bonds are equal.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** The negative charge is delocalised equally over two equivalent oxygens.

**Steps.** Two equivalent structures place the charge on each O; the hybrid has two identical bonds of order 1.5.

**✅ Answer.** The two contributors are **equivalent**, so the real ion has two identical C–O bonds (bond order 1.5) — the basis of carboxylic-acid acidity.

---

<a id="a8"></a>
### A8

> ❓ **Question.** Predict whether $S_N1$ is faster for tert-butyl bromide or methyl bromide, and why.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** $S_N1$ rate depends on carbocation stability.

**Steps.** tert-butyl gives a stable 3° carbocation; methyl would give an unstable methyl cation.

**✅ Answer.** **tert-butyl bromide** (faster $S_N1$, via a stable 3° carbocation).

---

<a id="a9"></a>
### A9

> ❓ **Question.** Rank the stability of radicals: methyl, ethyl, isopropyl, tert-butyl.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Radical stability parallels carbocations (hyperconjugation + +I).

**Steps.** $3° > 2° > 1° > \text{methyl}$.

**✅ Answer.** tert-butyl > isopropyl > ethyl > methyl.

---

<a id="a10"></a>
### A10

> ❓ **Question.** Is pyrrole aromatic? How many π electrons are in the ring?

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Cyclic, planar, conjugated; the N lone pair enters the ring π system.

**Steps.** Two C=C (4 π) + N lone pair (2) = 6 π electrons = $4(1)+2$.

**✅ Answer.** **Yes, aromatic** (6 π electrons, including the nitrogen lone pair).
