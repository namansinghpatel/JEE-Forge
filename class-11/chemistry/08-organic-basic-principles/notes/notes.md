## 🎯 Quick summary (the whole topic in a nutshell)

GOC ("General Organic Chemistry") is the **grammar of organic chemistry** — the principles every reaction
in Classes 11–12 is built on. Master this and the rest of organic becomes reasoning, not memorising.

**The seven things to really understand:**

1. **Hybridisation decides shape:** $sp^3$ (109.5°, alkanes), $sp^2$ (120°, alkenes), $sp$ (180°, alkynes).
   More s-character → shorter, stronger bonds and more acidic C–H.
2. **IUPAC naming:** prefixes + longest-chain root + suffix for the principal functional group, numbered for
   the lowest locant.
3. **Isomerism:** structural (chain, position, functional, metamerism, tautomerism) and stereo (geometrical
   cis–trans, optical).
4. **Bonds break two ways:** homolytic (→ radicals) or heterolytic (→ carbocation/carbanion). Know
   electrophiles (accept electrons) vs nucleophiles (donate).
5. **Electronic effects** redistribute electron density: inductive (through σ, short-range), resonance
   (through π, long-range, usually dominant), hyperconjugation (σ C–H into p/π).
6. **Stability orders:** carbocations 3° > 2° > 1° (resonance best); acidity = conjugate-base stability;
   basicity = lone-pair availability.
7. **Aromaticity (Hückel):** cyclic + planar + fully conjugated + $(4n+2)$ π electrons.

**The master trick (advanced):** translate every question into **"what stabilises the key species?"** — the
carbocation, the radical, the conjugate base, or the conjugate acid. Then reach for resonance → inductive →
hyperconjugation → s-character → solvation, in that order. And always check carbocation mechanisms for a
**1,2-shift** that gives a rearranged product.
