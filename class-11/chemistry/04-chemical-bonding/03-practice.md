# Chemical Bonding & Molecular Structure — Practice Range

> **35 practice problems**, graded **Basic (10) · Medium (15) · Advanced (10)**. Each has an **approach hint**
> (open after a real attempt) and a **collapsible full solution**. Topics: Lewis & formal charge, VSEPR,
> hybridization, bond parameters, MOT, dipole moment, resonance, hydrogen bonding, Fajans' rules.

> **Key tools:** FC $= V - L - \tfrac12 B$; steric number (σ + lone pairs) → hybridization; bond order $= \tfrac{N_b - N_a}{2}$; $\mu = q\,d$ (vector); H-bond needs H–F/O/N.

## 🔴 Advanced (P26–P35)

### P26
> Write the MO configuration of $\text{N}_2$ and give its bond order.

<details><summary>🧭 Approach</summary>

14 electrons; note the $\pi_{2p}$ below $\sigma_{2p}$ ordering for N₂.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Solution</summary>

$\sigma_{1s}^2\sigma^{*2}_{1s}\sigma_{2s}^2\sigma^{*2}_{2s}\pi_{2p}^4\sigma_{2p}^2$; $N_b = 10$, $N_a = 4$ → bond order 3.
</details>

---

### P27
> Compare bond orders of $\text{N}_2$ and $\text{N}_2^+$.

<details><summary>🧭 Approach</summary>

Remove one bonding electron.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Solution</summary>

$\text{N}_2$: 3. $\text{N}_2^+$: remove a $\sigma_{2p}$ electron → $N_b = 9$, bond order $= \tfrac{9-4}{2} = 2.5$.
</details>

---

### P28
> Shape and hybridization of $\text{ClF}_3$.

<details><summary>🧭 Approach</summary>

Cl: 3 bonds + 2 lone pairs = steric number 5.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Solution</summary>

$sp^3d$; two equatorial lone pairs give a **T-shaped** molecule.
</details>

---

### P29
> Explain why $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ has a higher boiling point than $\text{H}_2\text{S}$.

<details><summary>🧭 Approach</summary>

Compare hydrogen bonding.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Solution</summary>

O is small and very electronegative, so water has strong intermolecular H-bonding; S is larger/less electronegative, so $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ has only weak dipole forces. Hence $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ boils much higher.
</details>

---

<details><summary>🧭 Approach</summary>

Xe: 2 bonds + 3 lone pairs = steric number 5.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Solution</summary>

$sp^3d$; three equatorial lone pairs → **linear**.
</details>

---

### P31
> $\text{O}_2^{2-}$ (peroxide): find bond order and magnetism.

<details><summary>🧭 Approach</summary>

18 electrons; fill $\pi^*$ completely.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Solution</summary>

$N_b = 10$, $N_a = 8$ → bond order 1; all electrons paired → diamagnetic.
</details>

---

### P32
> Why is $p$-nitrophenol's boiling point higher than $o$-nitrophenol's?

<details><summary>🧭 Approach</summary>

Inter- vs intramolecular H-bonding.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Solution</summary>

The para isomer forms intermolecular H-bonds (association raises b.p.); the ortho isomer forms an intramolecular H-bond and does not associate.
</details>

---

### P33
> Rank the dipole moments: $\text{CO}_2$, $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, $\text{CHCl}_3$.

<details><summary>🧭 Approach</summary>

Symmetry decides cancellation.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Solution</summary>

$\text{CO}_2$ = 0 (linear, cancels) < $\text{CHCl}_3$ (polar, partial) < $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (bent, large $\mu$). So $\text{H}_2\text{O} > \text{CHCl}_3 > \text{CO}_2$.
</details>

---

### P34
> Explain, using MOT, why $\text{O}_2^+$ has a shorter bond than $\text{O}_2$.

<details><summary>🧭 Approach</summary>

Removing an antibonding electron raises bond order.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Solution</summary>

$\text{O}_2^+$ loses a $\pi^*$ (antibonding) electron, raising bond order from 2 to 2.5; higher bond order ⟹ shorter, stronger bond.
</details>

</details>

<details><summary>✅ Solution</summary>

$sp^3d$ with three equatorial lone pairs → **linear** ($180°$).
</details>
