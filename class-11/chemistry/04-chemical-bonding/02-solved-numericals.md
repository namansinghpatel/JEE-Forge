## 🟡 Medium (M1–M15)

<a id="m1"></a>
### M1
> ❓ **Question.** Calculate the formal charge on sulfur in $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$ (with two S=O and two S–O⁻, S having no lone pairs).

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** $\text{FC} = V - L - \tfrac12 B$; S has $V = 6$, $L = 0$, and 12 bonding electrons (6 bonds).

**Steps.** $\text{FC} = 6 - 0 - \tfrac12(12) = 6 - 6 = 0$.

**✅ Answer.** Formal charge on S $= 0$ (in the expanded-octet structure).

---

<a id="m2"></a>
### M2
> ❓ **Question.** Determine the hybridization of carbon in $\text{C}_2\text{H}_4$ (ethene).

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Steric number = σ-bonds + lone pairs on C.

**Steps.** Each C has 3 σ-bonds (2 C–H + 1 C–C) and 0 lone pairs → steric number 3 → $sp^2$.

**✅ Answer.** $sp^2$.

---

<a id="m3"></a>
### M3
> ❓ **Question.** Predict the shape of $\text{NH}_3$ and its bond angle.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Steric number = 3 bonds + 1 lone pair = 4 → tetrahedral electron geometry; one lone pair.

**Steps.** 4 electron pairs → tetrahedral arrangement; with one lone pair the shape is **pyramidal**, angle compressed to $107°$.

**✅ Answer.** Trigonal pyramidal, $107°$.

---

<a id="m4"></a>
### M4
> ❓ **Question.** Calculate the bond order of $\text{O}_2$ ($N_b = 10$, $N_a = 6$).

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Bond order $= \tfrac{N_b - N_a}{2}$.

**Steps.** $\tfrac{10 - 6}{2} = 2$.

**✅ Answer.** Bond order $= 2$.

---

<a id="m5"></a>
### M5
> ❓ **Question.** Why is $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ bent, not linear?

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Count lone pairs on O.

**Steps.** O has 2 bond pairs + 2 lone pairs (steric number 4). The two lone pairs repel strongly, bending the molecule to $104.5°$.

**✅ Answer.** Two lone pairs on oxygen bend it (bent, $104.5°$).

---

<a id="m6"></a>
### M6
> ❓ **Question.** Arrange $\text{C–C}$, $\text{C=C}$, $\text{C≡C}$ in order of increasing bond energy.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Bond energy increases with bond order.

**Steps.** Single < double < triple.

**✅ Answer.** $\text{C–C} < \text{C=C} < \text{C≡C}$.

---

<a id="m7"></a>
### M7
> ❓ **Question.** What is the hybridization and shape of $\text{PCl}_5$?

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Steric number = 5 σ-bonds + 0 lone pairs.

**Steps.** Steric number 5 → $sp^3d$ → trigonal bipyramidal.

**✅ Answer.** $sp^3d$, trigonal bipyramidal.

---

<a id="m8"></a>
### M8
> ❓ **Question.** Why does $\text{CO}_2$ have zero dipole moment though C=O bonds are polar?

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** $\mu$ is a vector sum; consider geometry.

**Steps.** $\text{CO}_2$ is linear and symmetric, so the two equal, opposite bond dipoles cancel.

**✅ Answer.** The bond dipoles cancel by symmetry → $\mu = 0$.

---

<a id="m9"></a>
### M9
> ❓ **Question.** Calculate the bond order of $\text{N}_2$ ($N_b = 10$, $N_a = 4$).

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Bond-order formula.

**Steps.** $\tfrac{10 - 4}{2} = 3$.

**✅ Answer.** Bond order $= 3$ (consistent with $\text{N}\equiv\text{N}$).

---

<a id="m10"></a>
### M10
> ❓ **Question.** Formal charge on the central oxygen in ozone $\text{O}_3$ (central O: 1 lone pair, one single + one double bond).

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** $V = 6$, $L = 2$, $B = 6$ (3 shared bonds).

**Steps.** $\text{FC} = 6 - 2 - \tfrac12(6) = 6 - 2 - 3 = +1$.

**✅ Answer.** $+1$ on the central oxygen.

---

<a id="m11"></a>
### M11
> ❓ **Question.** Predict the shape of $\text{SF}_4$.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Steric number = 4 bonds + 1 lone pair = 5.

**Steps.** $sp^3d$ (5 pairs, trigonal bipyramidal electron geometry); one lone pair in the equatorial plane gives a **see-saw** shape.

**✅ Answer.** See-saw ($sp^3d$).

---

<a id="m12"></a>
### M12
> ❓ **Question.** Which is more covalent: $\text{NaCl}$ or $\text{AlCl}_3$? (Fajans' rules)

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Higher cation charge + smaller size ⟹ more covalent.

**Steps.** $\text{Al}^{3+}$ is small and highly charged (high polarising power) vs $\text{Na}^+$, so $\text{AlCl}_3$ is more covalent.

**✅ Answer.** $\text{AlCl}_3$ (small, highly charged cation).

---

<a id="m13"></a>
### M13
> ❓ **Question.** How many σ and π bonds are in $\text{C}_2\text{H}_2$ (ethyne)?

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Triple bond = 1σ + 2π; each C–H is a σ.

**Steps.** 2 C–H σ + 1 C–C σ = 3σ; the C≡C contributes 2π.

**✅ Answer.** 3 σ-bonds and 2 π-bonds.

---

<a id="m14"></a>
### M14
> ❓ **Question.** Explain why $\text{HF}$ has a higher boiling point than $\text{HCl}$.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Consider hydrogen bonding (H–F).

**Steps.** HF forms strong intermolecular **hydrogen bonds** (F is small and very electronegative); HCl has only weaker dipole forces.

**✅ Answer.** HF has hydrogen bonding, raising its boiling point above HCl.

---

<a id="m15"></a>
### M15
> ❓ **Question.** State the hybridization of the central atom in $\text{SF}_6$.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Steric number = 6 σ-bonds + 0 lone pairs.

**Steps.** Steric number 6 → $sp^3d^2$.

**✅ Answer.** $sp^3d^2$ (octahedral).

## 🔴 Advanced (A1–A10)

<a id="a1"></a>
### A1
> ❓ **Question.** Write the MO electron configuration of $\text{O}_2$ and determine its bond order and magnetism.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** 16 electrons; fill $\sigma_{1s}\,\sigma^*_{1s}\,\sigma_{2s}\,\sigma^*_{2s}\,\sigma_{2p_z}\,\pi_{2p_x}\pi_{2p_y}\,\pi^*_{2p_x}\pi^*_{2p_y}$.

**Steps.** Bonding $N_b = 10$, antibonding $N_a = 6$; bond order $= \tfrac{10-6}{2} = 2$. The two $\pi^*$ electrons are unpaired (Hund) → paramagnetic.

**✅ Answer.** Bond order $2$; **paramagnetic** (2 unpaired electrons).

---

<a id="a2"></a>
### A2
> ❓ **Question.** Does $\text{He}_2$ exist? Use MOT.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** 4 electrons: $\sigma_{1s}^2\,\sigma^{*2}_{1s}$.

**Steps.** $N_b = 2$, $N_a = 2$; bond order $= \tfrac{2-2}{2} = 0$.

**✅ Answer.** Bond order $0$ → $\text{He}_2$ does **not** exist.

---

<a id="a3"></a>
### A3
> ❓ **Question.** Compare the bond orders and stabilities of $\text{O}_2$, $\text{O}_2^+$, $\text{O}_2^-$.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Add/remove electrons from the $\pi^*$ MOs; recompute bond order.

**Steps.** $\text{O}_2$: BO 2 (16 e⁻). $\text{O}_2^+$: remove a $\pi^*$ electron → $N_a = 5$, BO $= \tfrac{10-5}{2} = 2.5$. $\text{O}_2^-$: add one → $N_a = 7$, BO $= \tfrac{10-7}{2} = 1.5$.

**✅ Answer.** Stability/bond order: $\text{O}_2^+\,(2.5) > \text{O}_2\,(2) > \text{O}_2^-\,(1.5)$.

---

<a id="a4"></a>
### A4
> ❓ **Question.** $\text{NO}_3^-$ has three resonance structures. What is the average $\text{N–O}$ bond order?

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Average = (total bonds)/(number of N–O links).

**Steps.** Across resonance forms there are 4 bonds (one double + two single) shared over 3 N–O positions: bond order $= \tfrac{4}{3} \approx 1.33$.

**✅ Answer.** $\approx 1.33$ (all three N–O bonds equal by resonance).

---

<a id="a5"></a>
### A5
> ❓ **Question.** Why is the dipole moment of $\text{NH}_3$ ($1.47$ D) greater than that of $\text{NF}_3$ ($0.24$ D), though F is more electronegative than H?

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Add the lone-pair dipole to the bond dipoles as vectors.

**Steps.** In $\text{NH}_3$ the N–H bond dipoles and the lone-pair dipole point the **same way** (reinforcing). In $\text{NF}_3$ the N–F bond dipoles point **toward F**, opposing the lone-pair dipole, so they partly cancel.

**✅ Answer.** Reinforcing dipoles in $\text{NH}_3$ vs opposing dipoles in $\text{NF}_3$ → $\text{NH}_3$ has the larger $\mu$.

---

<a id="a6"></a>
### A6
> ❓ **Question.** Determine the hybridization of the central atom in $\text{XeF}_4$ and its shape.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Xe: 8 valence e⁻; 4 bonds + 2 lone pairs = steric number 6.

**Steps.** Steric number 6 → $sp^3d^2$ (octahedral electron geometry); two lone pairs occupy opposite (axial) positions → **square planar**.

**✅ Answer.** $sp^3d^2$, square planar.

---

<a id="a7"></a>
### A7
> ❓ **Question.** Rank bond angles: $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, $\text{NH}_3$, $\text{CH}_4$.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** More lone pairs ⟹ greater compression of the bond angle.

**Steps.** $\text{CH}_4$ (0 LP, $109.5°$) > $\text{NH}_3$ (1 LP, $107°$) > $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (2 LP, $104.5°$).

**✅ Answer.** $\text{CH}_4\,(109.5°) > \text{NH}_3\,(107°) > \text{H}_2\text{O}\,(104.5°)$.

---

<a id="a8"></a>
### A8
> ❓ **Question.** Calculate the formal charges in the two resonance structures of $\text{CO}_2$: (i) O=C=O and (ii) O≡C–O. Which is the major contributor?

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** FC $= V - L - \tfrac12 B$ for each atom.

**Steps.** (i) O=C=O: each O has $6 - 4 - 2 = 0$; C has $4 - 0 - 4 = 0$ → all zero. (ii) O≡C–O: triple-bonded O $= 6 - 2 - 3 = +1$; single-bonded O $= 6 - 6 - 1 = -1$; C $= 0$.

**✅ Answer.** Structure (i) O=C=O (all formal charges zero) is the major contributor.

---

<a id="a9"></a>
### A9
> ❓ **Question.** Explain why $o$-nitrophenol is more volatile (lower b.p.) than $p$-nitrophenol.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Compare intra- vs intermolecular hydrogen bonding.

**Steps.** $o$-nitrophenol forms an **intramolecular** H-bond (within the molecule), so it does not associate with neighbours. $p$-nitrophenol forms **intermolecular** H-bonds, associating molecules and raising the boiling point.

**✅ Answer.** Intramolecular H-bonding in the ortho isomer lowers its boiling point (more volatile).

---

<a id="a10"></a>
### A10
> ❓ **Question.** Using MOT, explain why the bond dissociation energy of $\text{N}_2$ is far greater than that of $\text{O}_2$.

> [!TIP]
> 🧭 **Strategy.** Compare bond orders.

**Steps.** $\text{N}_2$ has bond order 3 ($N_b - N_a = 6$); $\text{O}_2$ has bond order 2. A higher bond order means a shorter, much stronger bond, so $\text{N}_2$ needs far more energy to dissociate.

**✅ Answer.** $\text{N}_2$ (bond order 3) is much stronger than $\text{O}_2$ (bond order 2).
