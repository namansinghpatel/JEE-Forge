<a id="q1"></a>
### Q1
How many moles are in $54\ \text{g}$ of water?

<details><summary>💡 Hint (approach)</summary>

Grams → moles: divide by the molar mass. $M(\text{H}_2\text{O}) = 18$.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer & Solution</summary>

$n = \dfrac{54}{18} = \mathbf{3\ mol}$.
</details>

<a id="q11"></a>
### Q11
A compound is $92.3\%$ C and $7.7\%$ H by mass. Find its empirical formula.

<details><summary>💡 Hint (approach)</summary>

Take 100 g, convert each % (now grams) to moles, divide by the smaller. Expect a 1 : 1 ratio here.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer & Solution</summary>

C: $\tfrac{92.3}{12} = 7.69$; H: $\tfrac{7.7}{1} = 7.7$; ratio $\approx 1:1 \Rightarrow \mathbf{CH}$.
</details>

<a id="q12"></a>
### Q12
A compound with empirical formula $\text{CH}$ has molar mass $78$. Find its molecular formula.

<details><summary>💡 Hint (approach)</summary>

$n$ = molar mass ÷ empirical-formula mass (13), then multiply the empirical formula by $n$.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer & Solution</summary>

$n = \tfrac{78}{13} = 6 \Rightarrow \mathbf{C_6H_6}$ (benzene).
</details>

<a id="q17"></a>
### Q17
$4\ \text{mol H}_2$ and $1\ \text{mol N}_2$ react: $\text{N}_2 + 3\text{H}_2 \to 2\text{NH}_3$. Find the limiting reagent, moles of $\text{NH}_3$, and leftover.

<details><summary>💡 Hint (approach)</summary>

Divide each reactant's moles by its coefficient; smallest = limiting. Then base product and leftover on it.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer & Solution</summary>

$\text{N}_2: \tfrac{1}{1} = 1$; $\text{H}_2: \tfrac{4}{3} = 1.33$ ⟹ **$\text{N}_2$ limiting**. $\text{NH}_3 = 2$ mol. $\text{H}_2$ used $= 3$, left $= \mathbf{1\ mol}$.
</details>

### Q26
$32\ \text{g}$ of $\text{O}_2$ and $2\ \text{g}$ of $\text{H}_2$ react: $2\text{H}_2 + \text{O}_2 \to 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$. Find the mass of water and the mass of excess reactant left.

<details><summary>💡 Hint (approach)</summary>

Moles of each ($M_{\text{O}_2}=32$, $M_{\text{H}_2}=2$), then moles ÷ coefficient to find the limiting reagent; base water and leftover on it.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer & Solution</summary>

$n(\text{O}_2) = 1$, $n(\text{H}_2) = 1$. ÷ coeff: $\text{O}_2 = 1$, $\text{H}_2 = 0.5$ ⟹ **$\text{H}_2$ limiting**. Water $= 1$ mol $= \mathbf{18\ g}$. $\text{O}_2$ used $= 0.5$ mol, left $= 0.5$ mol $= \mathbf{16\ g}$.
</details>

### Q27
A hydrocarbon on combustion gives $2.64\ \text{g}$ $\text{CO}_2$ and $1.62\ \text{g}$ $\text{H}_2\text{O}$; molar mass $= 30$. Find the molecular formula.

<details><summary>💡 Hint (approach)</summary>

All C → $\text{CO}_2$, all H → $\text{H}_2\text{O}$. Find moles C and H, get the empirical ratio, then scale to molar mass.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer & Solution</summary>

$n(\text{C}) = \tfrac{2.64}{44} = 0.06$; $n(\text{H}) = 2\times\tfrac{1.62}{18} = 0.18$. Ratio $1:3 \Rightarrow \text{CH}_3$ (15). $n = \tfrac{30}{15} = 2 \Rightarrow \mathbf{C_2H_6}$.
</details>

### Q33
$24.6\ \text{g}$ of $\text{MgSO}_4\cdot x\text{H}_2\text{O}$ leaves $12\ \text{g}$ of anhydrous $\text{MgSO}_4$ ($M = 120$) on heating. Find $x$.

<details><summary>💡 Hint (approach)</summary>

Moles of $\text{MgSO}_4$ and moles of water lost separately; $x$ is their ratio.
</details>

<details><summary>✅ Answer & Solution</summary>

$n(\text{MgSO}_4) = \tfrac{12}{120} = 0.1$; water $= 24.6 - 12 = 12.6\ \text{g} \Rightarrow n = \tfrac{12.6}{18} = 0.7$; $x = \tfrac{0.7}{0.1} = \mathbf{7}$ (i.e. $\text{MgSO}_4\cdot7\text{H}_2\text{O}$, Epsom salt).
</details>
