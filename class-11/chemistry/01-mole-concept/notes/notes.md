## 🎯 Quick summary (the whole topic in a nutshell)

The mole concept is the "counting system" of chemistry. Atoms are far too small and too
numerous to count or weigh one at a time, so chemists use the **mole** — a fixed pack of
$6.022\times10^{23}$ particles — to connect the grams we can weigh to the number of particles
reacting.

**The five things to really understand:**

1. **A mole is just a number** — like a dozen (12) or a ream (500), but huge:
   $6.022\times10^{23}$. One mole of anything contains that many particles.
2. **Molar mass turns grams into moles.** A substance's molar mass (in g/mol) equals its
   atomic/molecular mass in u. So 18 g of water = 1 mole; divide mass by molar mass to get moles.
3. **Everything routes through moles.** To go from grams to number of particles, or grams to
   litres of gas, *always land on moles first* ($n = m/M$, then $\times N_A$ or $\times 22.4$ L).
4. **A balanced equation is a recipe in moles.** Its coefficients are mole ratios. The
   **limiting reagent** (the one that runs out first — found by moles ÷ coefficient) decides
   how much product forms.
5. **Concentration terms differ by their denominator.** Molarity uses litres of *solution*
   (and changes with temperature); molality uses kg of *solvent* (and doesn't).
