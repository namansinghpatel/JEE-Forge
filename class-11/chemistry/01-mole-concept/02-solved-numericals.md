## 🟢 Basic

<a id="b1"></a>
### B1 — mass to moles
**❓ Question.** How many moles are in $36\ \text{g}$ of water?

**Solution.** $M(\text{H}_2\text{O}) = 2(1) + 16 = 18\ \text{g/mol}$.
$$n = \frac{m}{M} = \frac{36}{18}$$

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{n = 2\ \text{mol}}$

---

<a id="b2"></a>
### B2 — moles to molecules
**❓ Question.** How many molecules are in $2\ \text{mol}$ of a gas?

**Solution.** $N = n\times N_A = 2\times6.022\times10^{23}$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{N = 1.204\times10^{24}\ \text{molecules}}$

---

<a id="b3"></a>
### B3 — moles to mass
**❓ Question.** Find the mass of $0.5\ \text{mol}$ of $\text{CO}_2$.

**Solution.** $M(\text{CO}_2) = 12 + 2(16) = 44\ \text{g/mol}$; $m = nM = 0.5\times44$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{m = 22\ \text{g}}$

<a id="b4"></a>
### B4 — moles of atoms
**❓ Question.** How many moles of atoms are in $23\ \text{g}$ of sodium?

**Solution.** $n = \dfrac{23}{23}$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{n = 1\ \text{mol of Na atoms}}$

### B5 — molar mass
**❓ Question.** Find the molar mass of $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$.

**Solution.** $2(1) + 32 + 4(16) = 2 + 32 + 64$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{M = 98\ \text{g/mol}}$

<a id="b6"></a>
### B6 — moles to gas volume
**❓ Question.** What volume does $0.25\ \text{mol}$ of a gas occupy at STP?

**Solution.** $V = n\times22.4 = 0.25\times22.4$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{V = 5.6\ \text{L}}$

**Solution.** $1\ \text{mol O}_2$ has $N_A$ molecules, each with 2 atoms: $2\times6.022\times10^{23}$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{1.204\times10^{24}\ \text{atoms}}$

## 🟡 Medium

<a id="m1"></a>
### M1 — empirical formula from % composition
**❓ Question.** A compound contains C $40\%$, H $6.7\%$, O $53.3\%$ by mass. Find its empirical formula.

**🧭 Strategy.** Take 100 g so percentages become grams, convert each to moles, then divide by the smallest to get the simplest whole-number ratio.

**Step 1 — grams → moles (per 100 g).**
$$\text{C}: \tfrac{40}{12} = 3.33, \quad \text{H}: \tfrac{6.7}{1} = 6.7, \quad \text{O}: \tfrac{53.3}{16} = 3.33$$
**Step 2 — divide by the smallest (3.33).**
$$\text{C}: 1, \quad \text{H}: 2, \quad \text{O}: 1$$

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{\text{CH}_2\text{O}}$

> **Exam tip:** If a ratio comes out like 1 : 1.5, multiply *all* by 2 (→ 2 : 3); for 1 : 1.33 multiply by 3. Never round 1.5 to 2.

---

<a id="m2"></a>
### M2 — molecular formula
**❓ Question.** A compound has empirical formula $\text{CH}_2\text{O}$ and molar mass $180\ \text{g/mol}$. Find its molecular formula.

**🧭 Strategy.** Molecular formula = (empirical) × $n$, where $n$ = molar mass ÷ empirical-formula mass.

**Step 1 — empirical formula mass.** $12 + 2 + 16 = 30$.
**Step 2 — find $n$.** $n = \dfrac{180}{30} = 6$.
**Step 3 — multiply.** $(\text{CH}_2\text{O})_6$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6}$ (glucose)

---

<a id="m3"></a>
### M3 — molarity
**❓ Question.** $4\ \text{g}$ of NaOH is dissolved to make $500\ \text{mL}$ of solution. Find the molarity.

**🧭 Strategy.** Molarity = moles of solute ÷ litres of solution. Convert grams to moles, mL to L.

**Step 1 — moles.** $M(\text{NaOH}) = 40$; $n = \tfrac{4}{40} = 0.1\ \text{mol}$.
**Step 2 — molarity.** $M = \dfrac{0.1}{0.5}$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{0.2\ \text{M}}$

---

<a id="m4"></a>
### M4 — moles of ions
**❓ Question.** How many moles of each ion, and total ions, are in $0.1\ \text{mol}$ of $\text{CaCl}_2$?

**🧭 Strategy.** Each formula unit dissociates into its ions in fixed ratio: $\text{CaCl}_2 \to \text{Ca}^{2+} + 2\text{Cl}^-$.

**Step 1.** $\text{Ca}^{2+}: 0.1\ \text{mol}$; $\text{Cl}^-: 2\times0.1 = 0.2\ \text{mol}$.
**Step 2.** Total ions $= 0.1 + 0.2$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{\text{Ca}^{2+}=0.1,\ \text{Cl}^-=0.2,\ \text{total}=0.3\ \text{mol}}$

---

<a id="m5"></a>
### M5 — gas volume from mass
**❓ Question.** What volume does $8\ \text{g}$ of $\text{O}_2$ occupy at STP?

**🧭 Strategy.** Mass → moles (÷ molar mass) → volume (× 22.4 L). Land on moles first.

**Step 1.** $n = \dfrac{8}{32} = 0.25\ \text{mol}$.
**Step 2.** $V = 0.25\times22.4$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{V = 5.6\ \text{L}}$

---

<a id="m6"></a>
### M6 — number of molecules
**❓ Question.** How many molecules are in $1.8\ \text{g}$ of water?

**🧭 Strategy.** Grams → moles → × $N_A$.

**Step 1.** $n = \dfrac{1.8}{18} = 0.1\ \text{mol}$.
**Step 2.** $N = 0.1\times6.022\times10^{23}$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{6.022\times10^{22}\ \text{molecules}}$

---

<a id="m7"></a>
### M7 — limiting reagent
**❓ Question.** $3\ \text{mol H}_2$ and $1\ \text{mol O}_2$ react: $2\text{H}_2 + \text{O}_2 \to 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$. Which is limiting, how much water forms, and what is left over?

**🧭 Strategy.** Divide each reactant's moles by its coefficient; the smallest ratio is limiting. Base the product on it.

**Step 1 — moles ÷ coefficient.** $\text{H}_2: \tfrac{3}{2} = 1.5$; $\text{O}_2: \tfrac{1}{1} = 1$. Smallest is $\text{O}_2$ ⟹ **$\text{O}_2$ is limiting**.
**Step 2 — product from $\text{O}_2$.** $1\ \text{mol O}_2 \to 2\ \text{mol H}_2\text{O}$.
**Step 3 — leftover.** $\text{H}_2$ used $= 2\times1 = 2\ \text{mol}$; left $= 3 - 2 = 1\ \text{mol}$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{\text{O}_2 \text{ limiting; } 2\ \text{mol H}_2\text{O}; 1\ \text{mol H}_2 \text{ left}}$

> **Exam tip:** More moles ≠ in excess. $\text{H}_2$ had the larger amount (3 mol) yet was in excess, because the recipe needs 2 of it per 1 $\text{O}_2$. Always use moles ÷ coefficient.

<a id="m8"></a>
### M8 — mass of product
**❓ Question.** $5.6\ \text{g}$ of $\text{N}_2$ reacts with excess $\text{H}_2$: $\text{N}_2 + 3\text{H}_2 \to 2\text{NH}_3$. Find the mass of ammonia formed.

**🧭 Strategy.** $\text{H}_2$ is in excess, so $\text{N}_2$ sets the yield. Moles of $\text{N}_2$ → moles of $\text{NH}_3$ via the 1 : 2 ratio → grams.

**Step 1.** $n(\text{N}_2) = \tfrac{5.6}{28} = 0.2\ \text{mol}$.
**Step 2.** $n(\text{NH}_3) = 2\times0.2 = 0.4\ \text{mol}$.
**Step 3.** $m = 0.4\times17$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{6.8\ \text{g NH}_3}$

---

<a id="m9"></a>
### M9 — molality
**❓ Question.** $18\ \text{g}$ of glucose ($M = 180$) is dissolved in $500\ \text{g}$ of water. Find the molality.

**🧭 Strategy.** Molality = moles of solute ÷ **kg of solvent** (not solution).

**Step 1.** $n = \tfrac{18}{180} = 0.1\ \text{mol}$.
**Step 2.** solvent $= 0.5\ \text{kg}$; $m = \dfrac{0.1}{0.5}$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{0.2\ \text{mol kg}^{-1}}$

---

<a id="m10"></a>
### M10 — mole fraction
**❓ Question.** A mixture has $2\ \text{mol}$ of A and $3\ \text{mol}$ of B. Find the mole fraction of A.

**🧭 Strategy.** Mole fraction = moles of that component ÷ total moles.

**Step 1.** $x_A = \dfrac{2}{2+3}$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{x_A = 0.4}$ (and $x_B = 0.6$; note $x_A + x_B = 1$)

---

<a id="m11"></a>
### M11 — percentage yield
**❓ Question.** A reaction's theoretical yield is $6.8\ \text{g}$ but only $5.1\ \text{g}$ is obtained. Find the % yield.

**🧭 Strategy.** % yield = actual ÷ theoretical × 100.

**Step 1.** $\dfrac{5.1}{6.8}\times100$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{75\%}$

---

<a id="m12"></a>
### M12 — average atomic mass
**❓ Question.** Chlorine exists as Cl-35 ($75\%$) and Cl-37 ($25\%$). Find its average atomic mass.

**🧭 Strategy.** Weighted average = Σ (fractional abundance × isotope mass).

**Step 1.** $0.75(35) + 0.25(37) = 26.25 + 9.25$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{35.5\ \text{u}}$

---

<a id="m13"></a>
### M13 — dilution
**❓ Question.** $100\ \text{mL}$ of $2\ \text{M}$ solution is diluted to $500\ \text{mL}$. Find the new molarity.

**🧭 Strategy.** Adding water does not change moles of solute: $M_1V_1 = M_2V_2$.

**Step 1.** $M_2 = \dfrac{M_1V_1}{V_2} = \dfrac{2\times100}{500}$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{0.4\ \text{M}}$

---

<a id="m14"></a>
### M14 — total atoms
**❓ Question.** How many atoms in total are in $9\ \text{g}$ of water?

**🧭 Strategy.** Moles of water → molecules → × (atoms per molecule). Water has 3 atoms per molecule.

**Step 1.** $n = \tfrac{9}{18} = 0.5\ \text{mol}$.
**Step 2.** atoms $= 0.5\times3\times6.022\times10^{23}$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{9.03\times10^{23}\ \text{atoms}}$

---

<a id="m15"></a>
### M15 — ppm
**❓ Question.** $5\ \text{mg}$ of a solute is present in $2\ \text{kg}$ of solution. Find the concentration in ppm.

**🧭 Strategy.** ppm = (mass of solute ÷ mass of solution) × $10^6$, with both masses in the same unit.

**Step 1.** $\dfrac{5\times10^{-3}\ \text{g}}{2\times10^{3}\ \text{g}}\times10^{6} = \dfrac{5\times10^{-3}}{2000}\times10^{6}$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{2.5\ \text{ppm}}$

## 🔴 Advanced

<a id="a1"></a>
### A1 — limiting reagent from masses
**❓ Question.** $28\ \text{g}$ of $\text{N}_2$ reacts with $10\ \text{g}$ of $\text{H}_2$: $\text{N}_2 + 3\text{H}_2 \to 2\text{NH}_3$. Find the mass of $\text{NH}_3$ and the mass of excess reactant left.

**🧭 Strategy.** Convert both to moles, divide by coefficients to find the limiting reagent, then base product and leftover on it.

**Step 1 — moles.** $n(\text{N}_2) = \tfrac{28}{28} = 1$; $n(\text{H}_2) = \tfrac{10}{2} = 5$.
**Step 2 — moles ÷ coefficient.** $\text{N}_2: \tfrac{1}{1} = 1$; $\text{H}_2: \tfrac{5}{3} = 1.67$. Smallest ⟹ **$\text{N}_2$ limiting**.
**Step 3 — product.** $n(\text{NH}_3) = 2\times1 = 2\ \text{mol} \Rightarrow m = 2\times17 = 34\ \text{g}$.
**Step 4 — leftover $\text{H}_2$.** used $= 3\times1 = 3\ \text{mol}$; left $= 5 - 3 = 2\ \text{mol} = 4\ \text{g}$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{34\ \text{g NH}_3; \ 4\ \text{g H}_2 \text{ left over}}$

<a id="a2"></a>
### A2 — molecular formula from combustion
**❓ Question.** On complete combustion, a hydrocarbon gives $0.88\ \text{g}$ $\text{CO}_2$ and $0.36\ \text{g}$ $\text{H}_2\text{O}$. Its molar mass is $42\ \text{g/mol}$. Find the molecular formula.

**🧭 Strategy.** All C ends up in $\text{CO}_2$, all H in $\text{H}_2\text{O}$. Find moles of C and H, get the empirical ratio, then scale to the molar mass.

**Step 1 — moles of C and H.**
$$n(\text{C}) = n(\text{CO}_2) = \tfrac{0.88}{44} = 0.02\ \text{mol}; \quad n(\text{H}) = 2\,n(\text{H}_2\text{O}) = 2\times\tfrac{0.36}{18} = 0.04\ \text{mol}$$
**Step 2 — empirical ratio.** $\text{C}:\text{H} = 0.02 : 0.04 = 1 : 2 \Rightarrow \text{CH}_2$ (empirical mass $= 14$).
**Step 3 — scale.** $n = \tfrac{42}{14} = 3 \Rightarrow (\text{CH}_2)_3$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{\text{C}_3\text{H}_6}$

<a id="a3"></a>
### A3 — percentage purity
**❓ Question.** $10\ \text{g}$ of impure $\text{CaCO}_3$ is heated: $\text{CaCO}_3 \to \text{CaO} + \text{CO}_2$. It releases $3.3\ \text{g}$ of $\text{CO}_2$. Find the % purity of the sample.

**🧭 Strategy.** Only the pure $\text{CaCO}_3$ gives $\text{CO}_2$. Work back from $\text{CO}_2$ moles to the mass of pure $\text{CaCO}_3$, then compare to the sample mass.

**Step 1.** $n(\text{CO}_2) = \tfrac{3.3}{44} = 0.075\ \text{mol}$.
**Step 2.** $n(\text{CaCO}_3) = 0.075\ \text{mol}$ (1 : 1); $m = 0.075\times100 = 7.5\ \text{g}$.
**Step 3.** purity $= \dfrac{7.5}{10}\times100$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{75\%\ \text{pure}}$

---

<a id="a4"></a>
### A4 — molarity from density and mass %
**❓ Question.** Concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ is $98\%$ by mass with density $1.84\ \text{g/mL}$. Find its molarity.

**🧭 Strategy.** Use $M = \dfrac{10\times d\times(\text{mass }\%)}{\text{molar mass}}$. (Derivation: take 1 L = 1000 mL; mass = 1000 d; solute mass = that × %/100; ÷ molar mass ÷ 1 L.)

**Step 1.** $M = \dfrac{10\times1.84\times98}{98}$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{18.4\ \text{M}}$

<a id="a5"></a>
### A5 — gas volume from a reaction
**❓ Question.** $6.5\ \text{g}$ of zinc reacts with excess dilute $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$: $\text{Zn} + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \to \text{ZnSO}_4 + \text{H}_2$. Find the volume of $\text{H}_2$ at STP.

**🧭 Strategy.** Zn → moles → moles $\text{H}_2$ (1 : 1) → × 22.4 L.

**Step 1.** $n(\text{Zn}) = \tfrac{6.5}{65} = 0.1\ \text{mol}$.
**Step 2.** $n(\text{H}_2) = 0.1\ \text{mol}$; $V = 0.1\times22.4$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{2.24\ \text{L H}_2}$

<a id="a6"></a>
### A6 — oxygen needed for combustion
**❓ Question.** Find the volume of $\text{O}_2$ at STP needed to completely burn $2\ \text{mol}$ of ethane: $2\text{C}_2\text{H}_6 + 7\text{O}_2 \to 4\text{CO}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O}$.

**🧭 Strategy.** Read the mole ratio straight from the balanced equation, then convert $\text{O}_2$ moles to volume.

**Step 1 — ratio.** $2\ \text{mol C}_2\text{H}_6$ needs $7\ \text{mol O}_2$.
**Step 2 — volume.** $V = 7\times22.4$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{156.8\ \text{L O}_2}$

<a id="a7"></a>
### A7 — mixing two solutions
**❓ Question.** $200\ \text{mL}$ of $0.5\ \text{M}$ and $300\ \text{mL}$ of $0.2\ \text{M}$ solutions of the same solute are mixed. Find the final molarity.

**🧭 Strategy.** Total moles (add moles from each) ÷ total volume in litres. Moles $= M\times V_{\text{L}}$.

**Step 1 — moles from each.** $0.5\times0.200 = 0.10$; $0.2\times0.300 = 0.06$. Total $= 0.16\ \text{mol}$.
**Step 2 — total volume.** $0.500\ \text{L}$; $M = \dfrac{0.16}{0.5}$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{0.32\ \text{M}}$

<a id="a8"></a>
### A8 — formula of a hydrate
**❓ Question.** $25\ \text{g}$ of hydrated copper sulphate $\text{CuSO}_4\cdot x\text{H}_2\text{O}$ leaves $16\ \text{g}$ of anhydrous $\text{CuSO}_4$ ($M = 160$) on heating. Find $x$.

**🧭 Strategy.** Find moles of $\text{CuSO}_4$ and moles of the water lost separately, then take their ratio — that ratio is $x$.

**Step 1 — moles of $\text{CuSO}_4$.** $\tfrac{16}{160} = 0.1\ \text{mol}$.
**Step 2 — water lost.** $25 - 16 = 9\ \text{g} \Rightarrow n(\text{H}_2\text{O}) = \tfrac{9}{18} = 0.5\ \text{mol}$.
**Step 3 — ratio.** $x = \dfrac{n(\text{H}_2\text{O})}{n(\text{CuSO}_4)} = \dfrac{0.5}{0.1}$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{x = 5 \ \Rightarrow\ \text{CuSO}_4\cdot5\text{H}_2\text{O}}$

<a id="a9"></a>
### A9 — counting ions and total particles
**❓ Question.** How many total ions are present in $11.1\ \text{g}$ of $\text{CaCl}_2$ ($M = 111$)?

**🧭 Strategy.** Mass → moles of $\text{CaCl}_2$ → each unit gives 3 ions ($1\,\text{Ca}^{2+} + 2\,\text{Cl}^-$) → × $N_A$.

**Step 1.** $n = \tfrac{11.1}{111} = 0.1\ \text{mol}$.
**Step 2.** ions per unit $= 3$; total ion moles $= 0.3$.
**Step 3.** $N = 0.3\times6.022\times10^{23}$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{1.807\times10^{23}\ \text{ions}}$

<a id="a10"></a>
### A10 — multi-step stoichiometry (mass to gas volume)
**❓ Question.** What volume of $\text{CO}_2$ at STP is produced when $25\ \text{g}$ of $\text{CaCO}_3$ ($M = 100$) is completely decomposed? $\text{CaCO}_3 \to \text{CaO} + \text{CO}_2$.

**🧭 Strategy.** Chain the bridge: mass of $\text{CaCO}_3$ → moles → moles $\text{CO}_2$ (1 : 1) → volume at STP. Land on moles between every step.

**Step 1.** $n(\text{CaCO}_3) = \tfrac{25}{100} = 0.25\ \text{mol}$.
**Step 2.** $n(\text{CO}_2) = 0.25\ \text{mol}$ (1 : 1).
**Step 3.** $V = 0.25\times22.4$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{5.6\ \text{L CO}_2}$
