## 🟢 Basic

<a id="b1"></a>
### B1 — solve by factorisation
**❓ Question.** Solve $x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0$.

**Solution.** Split the middle term: $x^2 - 2x - 3x + 6 = (x-2)(x-3) = 0$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{x = 2,\ 3}$

---

<a id="b2"></a>
### B2 — discriminant
**❓ Question.** Find the discriminant of $2x^2 + 3x + 1 = 0$ and state the nature of its roots.

**Solution.** $D = b^2 - 4ac = 9 - 8 = 1 > 0$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{D = 1;\ \text{two distinct real roots}}$

---

<a id="b3"></a>
### B3 — sum and product of roots
**❓ Question.** For $3x^2 - 6x + 2 = 0$, find the sum and product of the roots.

**Solution.** $\alpha+\beta = -\dfrac{b}{a} = \dfrac{6}{3} = 2$; $\alpha\beta = \dfrac{c}{a} = \dfrac{2}{3}$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{\alpha+\beta = 2,\ \ \alpha\beta = \tfrac23}$

---

<a id="b4"></a>
### B4 — pure quadratic
**❓ Question.** Solve $x^2 - 9 = 0$.

**Solution.** $x^2 = 9 \Rightarrow x = \pm 3$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{x = \pm 3}$

---

<a id="b5"></a>
### B5 — nature of roots
**❓ Question.** State the nature of the roots of $x^2 + x + 1 = 0$.

**Solution.** $D = 1 - 4 = -3 < 0$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{\text{two complex conjugate roots (no real root)}}$

---

<a id="b6"></a>
### B6 — form an equation from its roots
**❓ Question.** Form the quadratic equation whose roots are $2$ and $5$.

**Solution.** $x^2 - (\text{sum})x + (\text{product}) = x^2 - 7x + 10 = 0$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{x^2 - 7x + 10 = 0}$

---

<a id="b7"></a>
### B7 — quadratic formula
**❓ Question.** Solve $x^2 - 4x + 1 = 0$.

**Solution.** $x = \dfrac{4 \pm \sqrt{16 - 4}}{2} = \dfrac{4 \pm \sqrt{12}}{2} = 2 \pm \sqrt3$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{x = 2 \pm \sqrt3}$

---

<a id="b8"></a>
### B8 — find a constant from a known root
**❓ Question.** If $3$ is a root of $x^2 - 7x + k = 0$, find $k$ and the other root.

**Solution.** Substitute $x=3$: $9 - 21 + k = 0 \Rightarrow k = 12$. Product of roots $= k = 12$, so other root $= 12/3 = 4$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{k = 12,\ \text{other root} = 4}$

---

<a id="b9"></a>
### B9 — vertex / minimum value
**❓ Question.** Find the minimum value of $y = x^2 - 4x + 3$ and where it occurs.

**Solution.** Vertex at $x = -\dfrac{b}{2a} = \dfrac{4}{2} = 2$; $y = 4 - 8 + 3 = -1$. Since $a>0$, this is a minimum.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{y_{\min} = -1 \text{ at } x = 2}$

---

<a id="b10"></a>
### B10 — repeated root
**❓ Question.** Solve $x^2 - 2x + 1 = 0$.

**Solution.** $D = 4 - 4 = 0$, so a repeated root: $(x-1)^2 = 0$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{x = 1 \text{ (repeated)}}$

## 🟡 Medium

<a id="m1"></a>
### M1 — condition for equal roots
**❓ Question.** For what values of $k$ does $x^2 - kx + 9 = 0$ have equal roots?

**🧭 Strategy.** Equal roots means $D = 0$. Set the discriminant to zero and solve for $k$.

**Step 1 — write $D$.** $D = k^2 - 4(1)(9) = k^2 - 36$.
**Step 2 — set $D = 0$.** $k^2 = 36$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{k = \pm 6}$

---

<a id="m2"></a>
### M2 — value of $\alpha^2 + \beta^2$
**❓ Question.** If $\alpha,\beta$ are roots of $2x^2 - 5x + 1 = 0$, find $\alpha^2 + \beta^2$.

**🧭 Strategy.** $\alpha^2+\beta^2$ is symmetric — express it through sum and product, never by finding the roots.

**Step 1 — sum & product.** $S = \dfrac52$, $P = \dfrac12$.
**Step 2 — identity.** $\alpha^2+\beta^2 = S^2 - 2P = \dfrac{25}{4} - 1$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{\dfrac{21}{4}}$

---

<a id="m3"></a>
### M3 — equation with reciprocal roots
**❓ Question.** Form the quadratic whose roots are the reciprocals of the roots of $2x^2 + 3x + 4 = 0$.

**🧭 Strategy.** Replacing each root by its reciprocal is the same as **reversing the coefficients** (replace $x$ by $1/x$ and clear denominators).

**Step 1 — replace $x \to 1/x$.** $2/x^2 + 3/x + 4 = 0$.
**Step 2 — multiply by $x^2$.** $2 + 3x + 4x^2 = 0$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{4x^2 + 3x + 2 = 0}$

---

<a id="m4"></a>
### M4 — roots differing by a given amount
**❓ Question.** For $x^2 - 5x + k = 0$, the roots differ by $1$. Find $k$.

**🧭 Strategy.** Use $|\alpha-\beta| = \dfrac{\sqrt D}{|a|}$, so a known difference fixes $D$.

**Step 1.** $|\alpha-\beta| = 1 \Rightarrow \sqrt D = 1 \Rightarrow D = 1$.
**Step 2.** $D = 25 - 4k = 1 \Rightarrow 4k = 24$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{k = 6}$

---

<a id="m5"></a>
### M5 — range for real roots
**❓ Question.** Find the values of $k$ for which $x^2 + kx + 4 = 0$ has real roots.

**🧭 Strategy.** Real roots ⟺ $D \geq 0$. Solve the resulting inequality in $k$.

**Step 1.** $D = k^2 - 16 \geq 0$.
**Step 2.** $k^2 \geq 16 \Rightarrow |k| \geq 4$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{k \leq -4 \text{ or } k \geq 4}$

---

<a id="m6"></a>
### M6 — value of $\tfrac1\alpha + \tfrac1\beta$
**❓ Question.** If $\alpha,\beta$ are roots of $x^2 - 6x + 8 = 0$, find $\dfrac1\alpha + \dfrac1\beta$.

**🧭 Strategy.** $\dfrac1\alpha+\dfrac1\beta = \dfrac{\alpha+\beta}{\alpha\beta} = \dfrac{S}{P}$.

**Step 1.** $S = 6$, $P = 8$.
**Step 2.** $\dfrac{S}{P} = \dfrac{6}{8}$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{\dfrac34}$

---

<a id="m7"></a>
### M7 — build a rational quadratic from a surd root
**❓ Question.** One root of a quadratic with rational coefficients is $2 + \sqrt3$. Form the equation.

**🧭 Strategy.** Surd roots of a rational quadratic come in conjugate pairs, so the other root is $2 - \sqrt3$. Then use sum and product.

**Step 1 — conjugate.** Other root $= 2 - \sqrt3$.
**Step 2 — sum & product.** $S = 4$; $P = (2)^2 - (\sqrt3)^2 = 4 - 3 = 1$.
**Step 3.** $x^2 - Sx + P = 0$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{x^2 - 4x + 1 = 0}$

---

<a id="m8"></a>
### M8 — equal roots (find parameter)
**❓ Question.** For what $k$ does $2x^2 + kx + 2 = 0$ have equal roots?

**🧭 Strategy.** $D = 0$.

**Step 1.** $D = k^2 - 4(2)(2) = k^2 - 16 = 0$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{k = \pm 4}$

---

<a id="m9"></a>
### M9 — equation with roots $\alpha^2,\beta^2$
**❓ Question.** If $\alpha,\beta$ are roots of $x^2 + px + q = 0$, form the quadratic whose roots are $\alpha^2$ and $\beta^2$.

**🧭 Strategy.** Find the new sum and new product in terms of $p,q$, then write $x^2 - (\text{new sum})x + (\text{new product})=0$.

**Step 1 — given.** $S = \alpha+\beta = -p$, $P = \alpha\beta = q$.
**Step 2 — new sum.** $\alpha^2+\beta^2 = S^2 - 2P = p^2 - 2q$.
**Step 3 — new product.** $\alpha^2\beta^2 = P^2 = q^2$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{x^2 - (p^2 - 2q)x + q^2 = 0}$

---

<a id="m10"></a>
### M10 — biquadratic (quadratic in disguise)
**❓ Question.** Solve $x^4 - 13x^2 + 36 = 0$.

**🧭 Strategy.** Substitute $t = x^2$ to turn it into a quadratic, solve for $t$, then recover $x$.

**Step 1.** Let $t = x^2$: $t^2 - 13t + 36 = 0 \Rightarrow (t-4)(t-9) = 0 \Rightarrow t = 4, 9$.
**Step 2.** $x^2 = 4 \Rightarrow x = \pm 2$; $x^2 = 9 \Rightarrow x = \pm 3$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{x = \pm 2,\ \pm 3}$

---

<a id="m11"></a>
### M11 — maximum value
**❓ Question.** Find the maximum value of $5 + 4x - x^2$.

**🧭 Strategy.** $a = -1 < 0$, so the parabola opens down and has a maximum $= -\dfrac{D}{4a}$ at $x = -\dfrac{b}{2a}$.

**Step 1 — identify.** $a=-1,\ b=4,\ c=5$; $D = 16 + 20 = 36$.
**Step 2 — max value.** $-\dfrac{D}{4a} = -\dfrac{36}{-4} = 9$ (at $x = \dfrac{-4}{-2} = 2$).

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{\text{maximum} = 9 \text{ at } x = 2}$

---

<a id="m12"></a>
### M12 — one root is twice the other
**❓ Question.** If one root of $x^2 - 6x + k = 0$ is twice the other, find $k$.

**🧭 Strategy.** Let the roots be $\alpha$ and $2\alpha$; use sum to find $\alpha$, then product to find $k$.

**Step 1 — sum.** $\alpha + 2\alpha = 3\alpha = 6 \Rightarrow \alpha = 2$.
**Step 2 — product.** $k = \alpha\cdot 2\alpha = 2\alpha^2 = 2(4) = 8$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{k = 8}$ (roots $2$ and $4$)

---

<a id="m13"></a>
### M13 — value of $\alpha^3 + \beta^3$
**❓ Question.** If $\alpha,\beta$ are roots of $x^2 - px + q = 0$, find $\alpha^3 + \beta^3$ in terms of $p,q$.

**🧭 Strategy.** Use the identity $\alpha^3+\beta^3 = S^3 - 3SP$ with $S = \alpha+\beta$, $P = \alpha\beta$.

**Step 1.** $S = p$, $P = q$.
**Step 2.** $\alpha^3+\beta^3 = p^3 - 3pq$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{p^3 - 3pq}$

---

<a id="m14"></a>
### M14 — common root
**❓ Question.** Find $k$ if $x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0$ and $x^2 - 9x + k = 0$ have a common root.

**🧭 Strategy.** The first equation's roots are easy — find them, then substitute each into the second to get the possible $k$.

**Step 1.** Roots of the first: $x = 2, 3$.
**Step 2.** If $2$ is common: $4 - 18 + k = 0 \Rightarrow k = 14$. If $3$ is common: $9 - 27 + k = 0 \Rightarrow k = 18$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{k = 14 \text{ or } 18}$

---

<a id="m15"></a>
### M15 — range of a parameter for real roots
**❓ Question.** Find the values of $k$ for which $x^2 - 2(k+1)x + k^2 = 0$ has real roots.

**🧭 Strategy.** $D \geq 0$. Expand and solve the linear inequality in $k$.

**Step 1.** $D = 4(k+1)^2 - 4k^2 \geq 0 \Rightarrow (k+1)^2 - k^2 \geq 0$.
**Step 2.** $2k + 1 \geq 0 \Rightarrow k \geq -\dfrac12$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{k \geq -\tfrac12}$

## 🔴 Advanced

<a id="a1"></a>
### A1 — both roots positive
**❓ Question.** Find all $m$ for which both roots of $x^2 + (m-3)x + m = 0$ are positive.

**🧭 Strategy.** "Both roots positive (real)" needs **three** conditions together: $D \geq 0$, sum $> 0$, product $> 0$.

**Step 1 — product $> 0$.** $\alpha\beta = m > 0 \Rightarrow m > 0$.
**Step 2 — sum $> 0$.** $\alpha+\beta = -(m-3) = 3 - m > 0 \Rightarrow m < 3$.
**Step 3 — $D \geq 0$.** $(m-3)^2 - 4m \geq 0 \Rightarrow m^2 - 10m + 9 \geq 0 \Rightarrow (m-1)(m-9) \geq 0 \Rightarrow m \leq 1 \text{ or } m \geq 9$.
**Step 4 — intersect.** $0 < m < 3$ with ($m\leq1$ or $m\geq9$) gives $0 < m \leq 1$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{m \in (0,\ 1]}$

> **Exam tip:** "Both roots positive/negative/in an interval" is **always** a three-condition problem — discriminant, sum, product (or $a\cdot f(\text{point})$ for interval location). Missing the $D\geq 0$ check is the classic error.

<a id="a2"></a>
### A2 — power sum $\alpha^5 + \beta^5$
**❓ Question.** If $\alpha,\beta$ are roots of $x^2 - 2x + 4 = 0$, find $\alpha^5 + \beta^5$.

**🧭 Strategy.** Use the **recurrence** $p_n = S\,p_{n-1} - P\,p_{n-2}$, where $p_n = \alpha^n + \beta^n$, $S = \alpha+\beta$, $P = \alpha\beta$. Far faster than expanding.

**Step 1 — set up.** $S = 2$, $P = 4$; $p_0 = 2$, $p_1 = S = 2$.
**Step 2 — iterate.**
$$p_2 = 2(2) - 4(2) = -4, \quad p_3 = 2(-4) - 4(2) = -16$$
$$p_4 = 2(-16) - 4(-4) = -16, \quad p_5 = 2(-16) - 4(-16) = 32$$

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{\alpha^5 + \beta^5 = 32}$

---

<a id="a3"></a>
### A3 — range of a rational expression
**❓ Question.** Find the range of $y = \dfrac{x^2 + x + 1}{x^2 - x + 1}$ for real $x$.

**🧭 Strategy.** Treat it as a quadratic in $x$ and demand real $x$: cross-multiply, collect as $Ax^2+Bx+C=0$, and impose $D \geq 0$ in $y$.

**Step 1 — cross-multiply.** $y(x^2 - x + 1) = x^2 + x + 1 \Rightarrow (y-1)x^2 - (y+1)x + (y-1) = 0$.
**Step 2 — real $x$ needs $D \geq 0$.** $(y+1)^2 - 4(y-1)^2 \geq 0$.
**Step 3 — factor.** $[(y+1) - 2(y-1)][(y+1) + 2(y-1)] \geq 0 \Rightarrow (3 - y)(3y - 1) \geq 0 \Rightarrow \dfrac13 \leq y \leq 3$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{y \in \left[\tfrac13,\ 3\right]}$

---

<a id="a4"></a>
### A4 — a number lying between the roots
**❓ Question.** Find the values of $k$ for which $2$ lies between the roots of $x^2 - kx + k = 0$.

**🧭 Strategy.** A point $t$ lies **between** the roots of $f(x)=ax^2+\ldots$ iff $a\cdot f(t) < 0$.

**Step 1.** $f(2) = 4 - 2k + k = 4 - k$; here $a = 1 > 0$.
**Step 2.** Need $f(2) < 0 \Rightarrow 4 - k < 0$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{k > 4}$

---

<a id="a5"></a>
### A5 — symmetric pair with a common root
**❓ Question.** If $x^2 + ax + b = 0$ and $x^2 + bx + a = 0$ ($a \neq b$) have a common root, find the relation between $a$ and $b$.

**🧭 Strategy.** Subtract the two equations to eliminate $x^2$ and find the common root; then substitute back.

**Step 1 — subtract.** $(a-b)x + (b-a) = 0 \Rightarrow (a-b)(x-1) = 0$. Since $a \neq b$, the common root is $x = 1$.
**Step 2 — substitute $x=1$.** $1 + a + b = 0$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{a + b = -1}$

---

<a id="a6"></a>
### A6 — equation with roots $\alpha^4, \beta^4$
**❓ Question.** If $\alpha,\beta$ are roots of $x^2 - px + q = 0$, form the quadratic whose roots are $\alpha^4$ and $\beta^4$.

**🧭 Strategy.** Build the new sum and product from $S=p$, $P=q$, climbing powers via the identity for $\alpha^2+\beta^2$ first.

**Step 1.** $\alpha^2+\beta^2 = p^2 - 2q$; $\alpha^2\beta^2 = q^2$.
**Step 2 — new sum.** $\alpha^4+\beta^4 = (\alpha^2+\beta^2)^2 - 2(\alpha\beta)^2 = (p^2 - 2q)^2 - 2q^2$.
**Step 3 — new product.** $\alpha^4\beta^4 = q^4$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{x^2 - \left[(p^2 - 2q)^2 - 2q^2\right]x + q^4 = 0}$

---

<a id="a7"></a>
### A7 — equation with roots $\tfrac\alpha\beta, \tfrac\beta\alpha$
**❓ Question.** If $\alpha,\beta$ are roots of $x^2 - 6x + 2 = 0$, form the quadratic whose roots are $\dfrac\alpha\beta$ and $\dfrac\beta\alpha$.

**🧭 Strategy.** New sum $= \dfrac\alpha\beta + \dfrac\beta\alpha = \dfrac{\alpha^2+\beta^2}{\alpha\beta}$; new product $= 1$.

**Step 1.** $S = 6$, $P = 2$; $\alpha^2+\beta^2 = 36 - 4 = 32$.
**Step 2 — new sum.** $\dfrac{32}{2} = 16$. New product $= \dfrac\alpha\beta\cdot\dfrac\beta\alpha = 1$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{x^2 - 16x + 1 = 0}$

---

<a id="a8"></a>
### A8 — both roots negative
**❓ Question.** Find all $a$ for which both roots of $x^2 + 2(a+1)x + (9a - 5) = 0$ are negative.

**🧭 Strategy.** Both roots negative (real): $D \geq 0$, sum $< 0$, product $> 0$.

**Step 1 — product $> 0$.** $9a - 5 > 0 \Rightarrow a > \dfrac59$.
**Step 2 — sum $< 0$.** $-2(a+1) < 0 \Rightarrow a > -1$ (weaker than Step 1).
**Step 3 — $D \geq 0$.** $4(a+1)^2 - 4(9a-5) \geq 0 \Rightarrow a^2 - 7a + 6 \geq 0 \Rightarrow (a-1)(a-6) \geq 0 \Rightarrow a \leq 1 \text{ or } a \geq 6$.
**Step 4 — intersect.** $a > \dfrac59$ with ($a\leq1$ or $a\geq6$).

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{a \in \left(\tfrac59,\ 1\right] \cup [6,\ \infty)}$

---

<a id="a9"></a>
### A9 — quadratic inequality
**❓ Question.** Solve $x^2 - 5x + 6 < 0$.

**🧭 Strategy.** Find the roots, then use "a quadratic with $a>0$ is negative **between** its roots."

**Step 1 — roots.** $x^2 - 5x + 6 = (x-2)(x-3)$, roots $2$ and $3$.
**Step 2 — sign.** $a = 1 > 0$, so the expression is negative between the roots.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{2 < x < 3,\ \text{i.e. } x \in (2,3)}$

---

<a id="a10"></a>
### A10 — consecutive-integer roots
**❓ Question.** The roots of $x^2 - bx + c = 0$ are two consecutive integers. Show that $b^2 - 4c = 1$.

**🧭 Strategy.** Let the roots be $n$ and $n+1$; express $b$ and $c$ via sum and product, then compute $b^2 - 4c$.

**Step 1.** $b = n + (n+1) = 2n+1$; $c = n(n+1)$.
**Step 2.** $b^2 - 4c = (2n+1)^2 - 4n(n+1) = 4n^2 + 4n + 1 - 4n^2 - 4n = 1$.

**✅ Answer:** $\boxed{b^2 - 4c = 1}$ (the discriminant is a perfect square $=1$, as expected for integer roots differing by 1)
