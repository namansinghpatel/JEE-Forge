## 🎯 Quick summary (the whole topic in a nutshell)

Conic sections are the four curves you get by slicing a cone at different angles: **circle,
parabola, ellipse, hyperbola**. They all come from one rule — a point moves so that its distance to
a fixed **focus** is a fixed multiple $e$ (the **eccentricity**) of its distance to a fixed line
(the **directrix**).

**The five things to really understand:**

1. **Eccentricity picks the shape.** $e=0$ circle, $0<e<1$ ellipse, $e=1$ parabola, $e>1$
   hyperbola. If you know $e$, you know what you're dealing with.
2. **Each conic has a standard equation and a "package" of features.** Learn, for each, the
   focus, directrix, latus rectum and eccentricity in terms of its standard equation — then most
   questions are just reading off the right value.
3. **The circle is the easy one.** Centre $(-g,-f)$ and radius $\sqrt{g^2+f^2-c}$ from the general
   form; a line is tangent when the centre's perpendicular distance equals the radius.
4. **Ellipse and hyperbola are a mirror pair.** Same focus form $(\pm ae,0)$, same latus rectum
   $2b^2/a$; the only real differences are a sign ($1-e^2$ vs $e^2-1$), sum-vs-difference of focal
   distances, and the hyperbola's asymptotes. Learn them together.
5. **One tangent rule for everything:** "$T=0$" (split $x^2\to xx_1$, $y^2\to yy_1$) gives the
   tangent at a point and the chord of contact from an external point — same formula.

**The defining properties are the shortcuts:** parabola focal distance $=x+a$; ellipse
$PS_1+PS_2=2a$; hyperbola $|PS_1-PS_2|=2a$. Reach for these before any distance formula.
