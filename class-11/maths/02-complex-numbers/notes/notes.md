## 🎯 Quick summary (the whole topic in a nutshell)

Complex numbers **complete the number system** — every polynomial now has roots — and, crucially, they turn
**geometry into algebra** (rotations, loci) because each number is a point on the plane.

**The seven things to really understand:**

1. **The imaginary unit** $i = \sqrt{-1}$, $i^2 = -1$; powers of $i$ cycle with period 4. Careful:
   $\sqrt{-2}\sqrt{-3} = -\sqrt6$ (convert to $i$ first).
2. **Algebra:** add/subtract/multiply like binomials; **divide by multiplying by the conjugate** (since
   $z\bar z = |z|^2$ is real).
3. **Conjugate & modulus:** $\bar z = a - ib$, $|z| = \sqrt{a^2+b^2}$. Real iff $z = \bar z$; purely
   imaginary iff $z + \bar z = 0$.
4. **Argand plane & polar form:** $z = re^{i\theta}$; multiplication **multiplies moduli and adds
   arguments** (watch the quadrant of the argument!).
5. **De Moivre's theorem:** $(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta)^n = \cos n\theta + i\sin n\theta$ — powers, roots,
   and multiple-angle trig identities.
6. **Cube roots of unity:** $1 + \omega + \omega^2 = 0$, $\omega^3 = 1$ — a hugely useful identity; reduce
   powers of $\omega$ mod 3.
7. **Complex roots of quadratics** come in conjugate pairs when the discriminant is negative — closing the
   loop with the quadratics chapter.

**The master trick (advanced):** multiplying by $e^{i\theta}$ is a **rotation**, so "complex numbers as
geometry" — the **rotation formula** and reading $|...|$ as distance / $\arg(...)$ as angle — solves
equilateral-triangle, square and locus problems. The $n$-th roots of unity sum to zero and form a regular polygon.
